# Introduction

Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) spans hepatic steatosis, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), fibrosis and cirrhosis. Fibrosis is closely associated with clinical outcomes, yet liver biopsies assigned the same stage can differ substantially in their molecular and cellular composition (Kendall et al., 2023; Kamzolas et al., 2026). Hepatocyte metabolic changes, immune activation and stromal remodeling all contribute to the diseased liver. Understanding how these features relate to inherited susceptibility is an important step toward identifying disease mechanisms and choosing appropriate targets for intervention.

Human genetics and tissue profiling address different parts of this problem. Genome-wide association studies identify inherited variants associated with disease and related traits. Fine-mapping and colocalization with liver expression quantitative trait loci can connect some of these associations to candidate genes (Broadaway et al., 2024). However, a genetic association does not establish which gene mediates its effect, and some variants alter protein function without changing RNA abundance. Conversely, expression changes in diseased tissue can reflect altered cell composition, responses to injury or processes that sustain disease. A gene can therefore be informative about the established disease state without being an inherited risk factor. Distinguishing these possibilities is essential for deciding what an experiment should test.

Recent studies have expanded our understanding of MASLD through liver genetics, transcriptomics and functional genomics. Multicohort expression studies have identified disease-associated genes and pathways, while integrated clinical resources have linked liver expression to histology and outcomes (Kendall et al., 2023; Piras and DiStefano, 2024; Alvarez-Sola et al., 2026). Genetic studies have nominated regulatory candidates and distinguished components of inherited risk, and cell-resolved studies have identified regulatory effects that depend on cellular context (Jamialahmadi et al., 2024; Seagle et al., 2025; Hong et al., 2025; Gobeil et al., 2026a). Chromatin profiling and genetic perturbations have further connected disease-associated loci to candidate target genes (Saliba-Gustafsson et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2026). Together, these studies establish the value of combining molecular measurements. A remaining challenge is to determine, for any gene, what each measurement supports and which biological questions remain open.

Integrating these observations requires attention to what each assay can measure. Liver-enzyme associations provide information about liver biology but are not equivalent to direct evidence of MASLD susceptibility. Bulk RNA captures tissue remodeling but mixes changes in cellular abundance with changes within cells. Single-cell profiles provide lineage context, while protein and spatial measurements test whether RNA observations extend to another molecular layer or to intact tissue. Agreement can strengthen an interpretation, but shared source data and differences in assay coverage limit how far that agreement can be taken. An absent result is also difficult to interpret when the relevant gene, cell type or molecular mechanism could not be measured.

Here, we assemble a multimodal human MASLD resource that records inherited genetic evidence alongside gene expression in established disease. We combine liver-trait genetics with multicohort bulk RNA sequencing and use fixed gene programs, protein measurements, chromatin accessibility and spatial data to place the observations in cell and tissue context. Cross-sectional stage comparisons describe remodeling across fibrosis categories, while a molecular continuum adds resolution within recorded stages (Kamzolas et al., 2026; doi:10.1038/s42255-026-01543-7). We follow metabolic genes, including GNMT, MAT1A and CYP2C19, across RNA and protein measurements and examine stromal and ductular programs to distinguish tissue association from cellular origin. Genetic nominations remain provisional where variant identity and reference data require resolution, and atlas-derived estimates retain their qualification pending correction of repeated physical captures.

We organize the results in the MASLD Gene Catalog. Each entry distinguishes genetic support from disease-associated expression, records what could be tested and identifies an experiment that could resolve the remaining uncertainty. We also evaluate predictive models against relevant baselines and their prior exposure to the source data. By connecting molecular observations to explicit biological questions, this resource supports the design of functional studies that can distinguish inherited susceptibility, active disease processes and potential readouts of intervention.
