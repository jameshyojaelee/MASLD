### Units of inference

Participants or biological donors define replication throughout. Cells, spots, sequencing runs, guides, arrays, sections and repeated measurements never add to the biological sample size. Where a public deposit is keyed on sequencing run, we collapsed runs to donors with the recorded donor-pairing tables before fitting any model. Assay-specific sample totals, including repeated biopsies, were not summed into a total of unique people.

In the bulk RNA-seq cohorts, the unit is a source-qualified genetic-identity cluster with one selected library per cluster. Genotypes called from the RNA-seq reads identified repeated bulk libraries and conflicting sample labels. However, these RNA-based calls do not give complete identity confirmation, and calls in GSE193066 remain uncertain. Genetic-identity clusters are therefore not verified independent donors, and fits on source rosters are conditional reconstructions. An earlier identity sensitivity on the previous 844-record roster (seed 20260929) excluded ambiguous calls and every group with conflicting labels, and kept the lexicographically first eligible library in each concordant same-person component. It was evaluated only on continuum associations and is not a sensitivity of the 774-unit differential expression. A strict-depth identity sensitivity repeated the screen described below with read depth of at least 20 and at least 500 shared sites in each half of the sites. It keeps 738 clusters and is a four-cohort disease contrast, because GSE126848 keeps no disease unit under it (Supplementary Note 7). Inference that depends on these units requires the identity evidence to be resolved, because resampling cannot correct an incorrect biological unit.

Expression-based sex and PCA checks flag discrepancies. They do not establish genetic sample identity or justify relabeling participants. Genotypes were called from the RNA-seq reads at 8,660 autosomal sites, keeping calls with read depth of at least 10. Libraries with fewer than 1,000 called sites were not used. Two libraries were joined into one genetic-identity cluster when KING-robust kinship (PLINK 2.0) was at least 0.354 over at least 500 shared sites and also in each of two disjoint halves of the sites (at least 250 shared sites in each half). A cluster was excluded when any candidate same-person pair in it failed this rule or when its libraries carried conflicting source labels. Kinship was computed for every pair of the 1,466 libraries from ten cohorts in one joint panel, so the screen covered pairs within and across studies. All 72 pairs at or above 0.354 were within one cohort. Each pair of the 774 selected libraries shared at least 598 sites, and no pair reached 0.354. One pair from different cohorts, both disease units (GSE213621 and GSE130970), had kinship 0.288 (0.294 and 0.282 in the two halves) and opposite homozygous genotypes at 54 of 2,845 shared sites, against at most 6 in any pair at or above 0.354. This fits first-degree relatives, not one person, and both libraries were kept. No other pair of selected libraries reached kinship 0.0884 in PLINK. Because no pair from different cohorts reached 0.354, the screen has no positive example of a cross-cohort duplicate. KING also cannot distinguish monozygotic twins from repeated libraries of one person.

## Method details

### Bulk RNA-seq cohorts and quantification

The bulk analysis combines five control-bearing cohorts (GSE126848, GSE130970, GSE135251, GSE162694 and GSE213621). It uses 774 source-qualified genetic-identity clusters with one selected library each: 138 controls and 636 disease units. These clusters are not verified independent donors (see Units of inference). Paired-end fragments were aligned with STAR and quantified at gene level with reverse-stranded featureCounts against GENCODE v49 on GRCh38.p14 [ref: STAR] [ref: featureCounts]. Native fragments were recounted against the resolved gene annotation. The 855 annotations that could not be tested were counted but excluded from testing. Count and metadata columns were joined through the selected roster before any model was fitted. Fragment counts, individual read counts and single-cell UMIs are distinct measurement units and were never mixed. PRJNA512027 was kept as a provenance record only, because its library preparation is confounded with case-control status.

PCA and library-size quality control defined technical inclusion. Samples that failed only the expression-based sex check stayed eligible, and every bulk model adjusts for expression-inferred sex rather than the reported sex field. Under this rule, six disagreements between reported and inferred sex remained in the pooled model. [Supplementary denominator table; number to be assigned] reports cohort and sample keys, count-column alignment, overlapping QC flags and the losses at each analysis step. Denominators from earlier versions of this roster are listed in [supplementary table of earlier roster denominators; number to be assigned].

### Disease-versus-control differential expression

We fitted the primary model in the 774 genetic-identity clusters with limma-voom quality weights and the design `~ dataset + inferred_sex + group_binary`, with dataset as a fixed effect [ref: limma] [ref: voomWithQualityWeights]. Genes were filtered with `edgeR::filterByExpr` under `~ 0 + group_binary`, counts were RLE-normalized, and weights were estimated with `limma::voomWithQualityWeights` [ref: edgeR]. The complete tested family contains 24,115 Ensembl rows. A gene was called disease-associated when its Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) adjusted p was below 0.05 and |log2FC| exceeded 0.50. This rule gave 1,854 calls (1,333 higher and 521 lower in disease). As a comparator on the same fit, the effect-size-aware interval-null FDR gate (`limma::treat` at lfc = 0.25, BH FDR < 0.05) gave 2,131 calls. Cells and repeated libraries never enter as independent units.

We report both thresholds because a fold-change filter applied after a point-null test does not in general preserve FDR control within the filtered subset. It also does not establish that the true effect exceeds the chosen floor. The interval-null test builds its effect threshold into the test (McCarthy and Smyth, 2009). Its 0.25 threshold is distinct from the 0.50 threshold used for calls and display. Duplicate gene symbols were resolved without reference to outcome. Raw point-null results and ashr shrinkage estimates are sensitivity analyses only [ref: ashr].

GSE130970 healthy controls are the six biopsies reconstructed from Hoang et al. (2019) Supplementary Table 1, not every biopsy at fibrosis stage F0. The 774-unit model keeps these control labels. Results from fits with earlier control definitions are given in [supplementary table of fits with earlier control definitions; number to be assigned].

### Per-cohort disease models and GSE126848

Per-cohort disease models kept the pooled 24,115-gene universe and normalization, re-estimated voom quality weights within each cohort and used `~ inferred_sex + group_binary` when sex varied. A constant sex term was omitted. Missing sex, or a disease contrast that could not be estimated, was recorded as such. GSE126848 was modeled as `~ group_binary` in 30 all-male genetic clusters (23 control, 7 disease). We reproduced its 6,147 calls independently with a separate group-means basis and checked them against the native selected fragment counts. The identity-site screen removed 24 of 31 disease records, including all ten women in the source, and 2 of 25 controls. This unequal eligibility may limit how well the retained units represent the source cohort, and an estimable design does not resolve that. Retained disease libraries are shallower than controls. Figure 3C uses a refit after `filterByExpr` within the 30 units, with RLE factors recomputed and BH over the 15,592 retained genes, a different family from the fixed 24,115 genes of the other four cohorts (3,440 calls). Two sensitivities of the 6,147 calls are reported in the text (Supplementary Note 7) and are not used for the panel: refits of `~ group_binary + log2(library size)` with library size counted before or after RLE normalization, and the number of calls on genes with zero reads in all 30 libraries.

### Cross-cohort disease-effect transportability

We evaluated the existing cohort-specific disease-minus-control log2 fold changes
in a retrospective summary-data sensitivity. Within each cohort, inference uses
the selected genetic-identity clusters and the source model's inferred-sex term
where identifiable. Across cohorts, the cohort is the replication unit. Four
cohorts contribute the 24,115-gene universe; GSE126848 contributes its 15,592
native-filtered genes. The other 8,523 gene-by-cohort slots are untestable and
contribute no effect or precision. All 24,115 genes have four or five estimates.

The limma empirical-Bayes t statistic gives a moderated SE as log2FC/t. Five
exact source refits verified it against `stdev.unscaled * sqrt(s2.post)`.
We fitted an intercept-only random-effects model by restricted maximum
likelihood (metafor 4.8.0), with modified Hartung–Knapp uncertainty: the variance
factor is at least one and the reference t distribution has k−1 degrees of
freedom. These are empirical-Bayes precision approximations, not raw independent
sampling variances. BH correction covers all 24,115 mean-effect hypotheses;
non-estimable tests would enter correction as p=1 while remaining untestable.
The existing 1,854 pooled calls were retained as a fixed descriptive subset.
Nominal Cochran Q p values were not used to declare corrected heterogeneity
discoveries. Prediction intervals were exploratory and conditional on an
exchangeable future cohort, reported only for k=5 and positive estimated
between-cohort variance. Their future-cohort coverage was not validated.

The specification was recorded before these new fits, after the underlying
cohort outcomes had already been observed. Direction agreement and cohort
omission are therefore discovery-data robustness checks, not independent
replication. The separate SteatoSITE stage contrasts retain their original
prespecification, donor-based uncertainty and hypothesis families. They test
cross-sectional stage effects rather than the disease-versus-control estimand.

### Postmortem liver fat in GTEx

In GTEx v8 liver [ref: GTEx v8], fat was measured on each donor's H&E slide as the clear-vacuole area fraction of the tissue. Thresholds were set before any expression result was read. The gene family, set before the run, was the 1,002 disease-versus-control calls present in GTEx (GENCODE v26) that pass edgeR `filterByExpr` on GTEx liver counts. For each gene, limma `voomWithQualityWeights` (weights from the covariate-only design) and `lmFit` estimated the slope on fat rank in 222 donors with `~ fat_rank + age_group + sex + Hardy scale + ischemic time + RIN + collection center`. The primary statistic was the Spearman correlation across genes between this slope and the bulk disease-versus-control log2 fold change. It was tested two-sided by Freedman-Lane permutation within six strata of center by Hardy group (10,000 draws) [ref: Freedman-Lane permutation]. Two qualifiers formed a Holm family of two: the same correlation in the 138 donors with Hardy scale 1 to 4 (deaths not on a ventilator), and the difference between the fat and ischemic-time correlations in a joint model (2,000 bootstrap resamples). Positive controls were PLIN2 and 197 steatosis-associated genes from the bulk cohorts that are not disease-versus-control calls. Negative controls were slide tissue area and ischemic time. After the result, a post hoc sensitivity added the slide tissue-area rank as a covariate, with quality weights re-estimated on the covariate-only design that includes it. The area-adjusted and primary correlations were compared on the same 2,000 stratified donor-bootstrap resamples (six center-by-Hardy strata, weights fixed). Donor resampling shifts each correlation toward 0 (bootstrap mean 0.577 against the observed 0.638), so only the paired difference is reported. Donors are the unit of replication. The seed was 20260923.

### Adolescent liver cohorts

GSE66676 provides liver expression (Affymetrix HuGene 1.0 ST) from 67 adolescents with severe obesity, sampled at bariatric surgery [ref: GSE66676 source study]. The signature was the 1,854 disease-versus-control calls, of which 881 up and 183 down gene symbols map to array probes. Each probe was z-scored against the No NAFLD group, z was averaged over the probes of each symbol, and the score was the mean z of up symbols minus the mean z of down symbols. We removed two participants whose recorded sex did not match their expression-inferred sex, leaving 32 with NAFLD and 33 without. The single prespecified test (no correction) was the NAFLD coefficient in `score ~ nafld + sex + age + bmi` (residual df 60). We checked it by Freedman-Lane permutation (10,000 draws) and report it as an adjusted standardized difference d with its 95% interval. We computed the minimum detectable d at 80% power for the analyzed n. Random gene sets matched on No NAFLD SD decile served as negative controls. GSE185051 (52 children) was used only descriptively, by correlating the score with NAS [ref: GSE185051 source study]. Participants are the unit of replication. The seed was 20260923.

### Composition sensitivity of disease-associated genes

We refitted the 774-unit disease-versus-control model with estimated cell-type fractions added as log-ratio coordinates. We used four prespecified codings (16 cell types with a floor of 1e-6 or 1e-4; seven lineage groups; and a five-group coding in the three cohorts where it resolves stroma, 537 units) and two fraction inputs (InstaPrism estimates under the BayesPrism model, from the fragment counts used here and from an earlier count matrix of the same cohorts), giving eight combinations. Ratios of adjusted to unadjusted log2 fold change were summarized with 1,000 bootstrap draws that resampled units within cohort-by-group strata (seed 20260923). Per-gene labels required four checks to pass. Both positive controls had to pass (12 Stromal ECM calls and 159 stromal-specific calls, each with a median ratio below 0.5 in every combination). The negative control had to pass (52 calls expected to change uniformly, median ratio at least 0.7 in every combination). A within-hepatocyte benchmark and a calibration null (lambda at most 1.2) also had to pass. The stromal-specific positive control did not pass (median ratio 0.528 in one combination). The uniform-change negative control also failed in seven of the eight combinations (median ratios 0.484 to 0.695), the within-hepatocyte benchmark failed, and the calibration null exceeded its limit in one combination (lambda 1.254). No per-gene labels were produced.

### Genetic ancestry sensitivity

Genetic ancestry principal components (PC1 to PC4) were computed from RNA-called genotypes projected onto 1000 Genomes founders, and attached by run identifier to the 774 genetic-identity clusters (138 control, 636 disease) [ref: 1000 Genomes]. We refitted the stored filtered, RLE-normalized DGEList in two ways. T1 used `~ dataset + inferred_sex + PC1 + PC2 + PC3 + PC4 + group_binary`. T2 used the primary design in the 614 clusters called EUR (97 control, 517 disease). limma `voomWithQualityWeights`, `lmFit` and `eBayes` were rerun, and calls used BH over the 24,115-gene universe with adjusted p < 0.05 and |log2FC| > 0.5. The statistic was the number of the 1,854 primary calls that changed status (lost plus gained).

The T1 reference was 200 refits with the four PC columns permuted jointly within each of the 10 cohort-by-group cells, which keeps the PC imbalance between groups. The T2 reference was 100 refits on random subsets drawn within cells to the EUR counts. One-sided empirical p values used (1 + k)/(B + 1), and the family F1 = {T1, T2} was Holm-corrected at family-wise alpha 0.05. Controls were reproduction of the primary fit; the rs738409 G allele frequency by ancestry call (exact CMH test stratified by disease group); a joint F-test of the four PCs in a cohort-by-group cell-means fit, compared between true and permuted PCs; and GSTM1 as a gene expected to differ by ancestry. A gene was labeled ancestry-sensitive, descriptively and without multiplicity correction, if it changed status with the PCs and in at most 9 of 200 permuted fits. We estimated the expected number of false labels by applying the same rule to each permuted fit against the other 199. The seed was 20260923 (L'Ecuyer-CMRG), and all permutations and subsets were drawn before any fit.

### Spike-in calibration of bulk evidence states

We added known disease-versus-control changes to the 774-unit fragment counts (79,095 genes) by binomial thinning (Gerard, 2020). Each count in the group to be lowered was replaced by Binomial(y, 2^-|delta|). In each of 50 replicates, disease labels were first permuted within cohort, keeping each cohort's control and disease counts. We then drew 320 genes per AveExpr decile of the primary fit from the 24,115-gene universe. These genes received uniform changes of 0, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0 and 1.5 log2FC; heterogeneous changes of 1.0 and 0.5 log2FC with between-cohort SD 0.306 (the median DerSimonian-Laird tau among the primary calls); or changes of 1.0 and 2.0 log2FC in one cohort only.

Each replicate was refit with the full primary procedure (`filterByExpr`, RLE, `voomWithQualityWeights`, `~ dataset + inferred_sex + group_binary`, BH over the replicate's universe), together with the five leave-one-cohort-out refits and the five per-cohort fits. A supported call was the primary disease-versus-control call. Source dependence was loss of the call, with the pooled sign, in at least one leave-one-cohort-out refit. We also scored an equivalence rule (DerSimonian-Laird random effects, Hartung-Knapp SE, TOST at margin 0.5 with t on 4 df, BH), but it defines no evidence state. Inside each simulated fit, the unit is the genetic-identity cluster. Spiked genes within replicates are calibration draws, not donors. There is no hypothesis test and no family. Each rate carries a 2,000-draw bootstrap interval that resamples the 50 replicates, reported beside a Clopper-Pearson interval. A threshold certifying a low error rate was met only when the unfavorable bootstrap bound was on the correct side. The pooled, leave-one-cohort-out and per-cohort fits were reproduced before any replicate was run. Stop rules covered the null background (median unspiked supported calls above 105) and thinning size. Seeds were 20260923 + r for replicate r (Mersenne-Twister) and 20261923 for the bootstrap.

### lncRNA annotation and disease association

Versioned Ensembl identifiers from the GENCODE v49 annotation used for counting were the identity key for protein-coding genes and lncRNAs. Symbols were display aliases only. The primary lncRNA analysis excluded small RNAs, circRNAs, pseudogenes and transcript-isoform claims. The five pooled cohorts had to share the same reverse-stranded featureCounts setting. We checked strand orientation from deposited STAR summaries and, for GSE162694, from a deterministic recount of ten BAM files with featureCounts modes 0, 1 and 2. The cohort-level criterion required a median reverse-to-same assignment ratio of at least four, at least 90% of checked libraries at four or above, and every library above one. GSE213621 also required that no clinical stage group have more than 10% of libraries below four, and that the largest between-group difference in failure fraction be at most five percentage points. No sample was excluded on the basis of this check.

lncRNA tests share the 24,115-gene pooled family, including the primary and interval-null results; we did not calculate a separate lncRNA-only FDR. Cohort-specific, leave-one-cohort-out and equal-library-weight fits were prespecified robustness analyses. The secondary association between biotype and disease-association status used a gene-level logistic model adjusted for average expression, expression variability, log gene length and the number of cohorts in which the gene was detected. Genes are comparison units in this descriptive analysis, not biological replicates. For the direction comparison, each disease-associated lncRNA was matched 1:1 without replacement to a protein-coding gene on these covariates. The caliper was chosen without reference to outcome as the first of 0.20, 0.15, 0.10 and 0.05 SD at which all standardized differences met the 0.10 limit. In the 774-unit analysis, the 0.20-SD match failed this balance rule, so 0.15 SD was selected, giving 520 pairs. Gene-level matching is descriptive.

Each lncRNA was assigned one deterministic genomic-context class from exon overlap, gene-body overlap, strand and opposite-strand TSS proximity to protein-coding genes, applied in that order of priority. Same-strand exonic-overlap and complex mappings stayed in coverage tables but could not become named examples. Genomic context was not interpreted as evidence that the mature RNA molecule is functional.

Prespecified Hotspot programs with at least 5% lncRNA L1 weight were rescored after removal of all uniquely mapped lncRNA members. Module membership of other genes was not changed, and modules were not rediscovered. The exact original cell scores had been saved, but the original neighbor graph had not. We therefore reconstructed the source graph from the original cell-type matrix, source filters, Hotspot model, neighbor count and gene filter. The exact stored score was kept as the baseline, and only the reconstructed gene-removal difference was added to it. The technical reconstruction required a correlation of at least 0.995 with the stored all-gene score. This threshold was set during source checking and kept separate from biological acceptance. Scores were collapsed to biological donor. A sensitivity passed when donor-level Pearson and Spearman correlations were both at least 0.90 and the coefficient direction in the prespecified model `score ~ stage_ordinal + dataset` was preserved.

### Fibrosis-stage and NAS contrasts

We compared adjacent fibrosis stages and adjacent NAS groups cross-sectionally in the five-cohort fragment-count data. Fibrosis contrasts used the three cohorts with an exact F0–F4 fibrosis stage (GSE130970, GSE135251 and GSE162694), with each contrast drawn from 387 staged units. GSE213621 deposits only F0–F1, F2 and F3–F4 bins and was not used in these contrasts. Each analysis kept one library per selected genetic-identity cluster and only the cohorts that carried both compared groups. The design was `~ dataset + inferred_sex + group` when the multi-cohort terms were estimable. Counts were normalized with edgeR and analyzed with `limma::voomWithQualityWeights`. Empirical-Bayes p values were BH-adjusted separately across the complete 24,115-gene family for every contrast. No stage-specific fold-change threshold was applied. Direct F1, F2, F3 and F4 versus F0 models used the same data and covariates. For every model we saved sample count, cohort count, design rank, coefficient name and the complete family, and the five-cohort models passed checks of design rank, family size and FDR recomputation. Arrows in compact panel labels denote ordered cross-sectional contrasts between different participants, who were not sampled repeatedly over time. Raw counts of differentially expressed genes are not treated as comparable effect sizes when sample size or cohort availability differs among contrasts.

The F0 reference combines 14 source controls and 89 disease biopsies (103 clusters). The adjacent F0→F1, F1→F2, F2→F3 and F3→F4 contrasts contain 205, 190, 157 and 94 clusters, and no cluster is on both sides of a contrast. We report this reference with two sensitivities: one removes controls from F0 only, and one adds a control-status term at unchanged sample size. The disease-only-F0 rule does not remove controls at other stages. Contrasts that could not be estimated are recorded as such. Early-stage GNMT and MAT1A claims that do not survive these comparisons are not retained. As a further sensitivity, every fibrosis contrast was refitted with each cohort left out in turn under the same design, and F1 versus F0 was fitted in each cohort alone (`~ inferred_sex + group`). Cohort heterogeneity of F1 and F4 versus F0 was tested with a cohort-by-stage interaction (`~ inferred_sex + dataset + dataset:group`), using a 2-df moderated F test with BH correction over 24,115 genes. ### Grouped-stage comparison in GSE213621

GSE213621 records fibrosis only in source bins (F0–F1, F2, F3–F4). To compare it with the exact-stage cohorts, we refitted GSE130970, GSE135251 and GSE162694 on the same bins in disease units only (371 genetic-identity clusters: 65, 197 and 109). This fit used limma `voomWithQualityWeights` on the stored counts and RLE factors with the design `~ dataset + inferred_sex + bin`. GSE213621 was fitted in its 258 disease units (F0–F1 87, F2 94, F3–F4 77) with `~ inferred_sex + bin`, and its 68 controls were excluded. For each contrast (F3–F4 and F2, each against F0–F1), the statistic b was the ordinary least-squares slope of the GSE213621 log2 fold change on the discovery log2 fold change, over discovery BH calls kept in GSE213621. The null permuted bin labels 1,000 times within eight strata of GEO accession quartile by inferred sex, so it keeps submission order and sex structure. P values are one-sided, and the two contrasts form one Holm family (family-wise alpha 0.05). Intervals come from 200 bootstrap draws stratified by bin and sex. The normal interval is primary, and the percentile interval is reported beside it. A contrast was untestable if the minimum detectable slope under the null (2.80 times the null SD) exceeded 0.30, or if fewer than three of the retained positive controls (COL1A1, EPCAM, GNMT, LUM, CYP2C19) had the predicted sign at nominal p < 0.05.

We read effect size from a ratio. Each discovery cohort was held out in turn and the other two were refitted. The mean GSE213621 slope on those refitted effects was then divided by the mean slope of the held-out cohort on the same effects and genes. Similar size required the 95% interval of the ratio to lie within 0.67 to 1.5. The negative control was the slope of the GSE213621 male-versus-female coefficient on the discovery effects over autosomal genes. It fails if its interval excludes 0 and its absolute value exceeds 0.10. Outside the test family, sensitivities added GEO accession quartile, RLE factors recomputed after filtering, a within-cohort top-50-gene share covariate, omission of GSE162694, a women-only fit (161 units), and six isometric log-ratio coordinates of seven lineage groups from the fragment-count deconvolution fractions added to both fits. Per-gene states used BH within the retained genes of each passing contrast. The seed was 20260923. In this analysis, the minimum-detectable-slope rule made F2 untestable, and the negative control failed. The GSE213621 F3–F4 versus F0–F1 contrast had been fitted, and its sign agreement read, before this analysis was specified, so the analysis is not blind.

### Continuum-linked direction, programs and readouts

This extension asks two questions. Do inherited expression associations and tissue remodeling point to different experiments? Which measurements could describe molecular variation within an existing histologic eligibility group? The analysis is exploratory. It does not change genetic memberships or Gene Catalog evidence classes. It makes no recommendation for clinical practice, does not estimate treatment benefit and does not set a clinical cutoff. The continuum and its sliding-window adaptation are those of Kamzolas et al. (2026).

*Signed inherited direction.* The signal-level direction analysis used an earlier version of the 50-study colocalization results (2026-08-17; 462 SuSiE genes), not the corrected rerun that gives the 436 SuSiE genes reported elsewhere in this paper. We kept all 5,362 exported SuSiE signal pairs and all 22,356,697 variant posterior records from 816 main-trait gene–study pairs. Signal support used the same strict PP.H4 > 0.5 threshold as the colocalization analysis. Direct MASLD, liver fat, liver enzymes and other traits were kept as separate fields, as were ancestry and the GWAS and eQTL signal identifiers. Supported gene–trait records elsewhere in the portfolio, including ABF-only sensitivity support, stayed visible when signal posteriors were unavailable. They did not take a direction from an unrelated lead variant. Rows without a gene identifier were exported separately and could not be joined to a named Gene Catalog record. Main-trait and full-portfolio testing denominators were recorded separately, distinguishing missing SuSiE tests from tests without support. These posterior support thresholds are not frequentist p values.

At matched GRCh37 chromosome and position, we matched the eQTL effect and other allele pair to the recorded GWAS effect and other allele pair. An exact swap negated the eQTL beta. Unmatched alleles, non-SNV alleles, ambiguous duplicate effects, missing effects and palindromic variants were left direction-unresolved. We did not infer strand from an effect sign. This palindrome rule affects direction only and does not remove variants from the colocalization analysis. For each shared-variant posterior vector, the sign of GWAS beta × aligned eQTL beta describes expression associated with a higher value of the named trait. Only for direct disease phenotypes do we describe this as expression associated with disease risk. We summed the original shared posterior mass carrying each sign and required at least 0.95 of the original total in one resolved direction. Missing or ambiguous mass was not removed by renormalization. This is a declared descriptive direction criterion, not a calibrated posterior probability that expression mediates the trait.

The available signed effects are marginal summary-statistic effects and do not identify the conditional effects of individual SuSiE signals. We therefore resolved directional perturbation records only for loci with one exported GWAS signal and one exported eQTL signal. Records with multiple exported signals keep marginal-direction evidence but remain signal-direction unresolved. Even a single exported pair does not exclude additional undetected signals. No instrumental-variable effect or drug effect was estimated. Per-study GWAS sample sizes and per-variant eQTL sample sizes accompany the signed evidence. We also tabulated consistency among resolved records for the same gene, named trait and ancestry across studies. Conflicting signs remain in their individual records and suspend a common directional perturbation hypothesis. Consistent signs across studies are not called independent replication, because participant overlap between studies is unresolved. Different signal locations within a gene remain distinct possible mechanisms, not a pooled estimate.

*Expression relationship.* This extension was run on an earlier 23,370-gene version of the bulk testing family, not the 24,115-gene family of the primary fit. We joined inherited evidence to that complete family and kept genetics-only genes outside that measured universe. A matching or opposing direction required the two-axis, two-cohort continuum-support rule. Genes with inherited support but no supported remodeling were kept, and genes not observable in bulk were distinguished from tested genes. On that 23,370-gene basis, the 139 observed axis genes keep their separate leave-one-out results and 139-gene BH family; in the 24,115-gene fit this family has 143 genes. They do not acquire ordinary continuum support from the axis they define. Opposing directions motivate experiments that test both directions and alternative explanations; they do not show compensation. Lifelong genetic associations do not establish the sign or timing of a pharmacological effect. Gene Catalog annotation tables keep separate fields for the proposed transcript, the perturbation hypothesis, cell-context links, the disease-associated profile, the proposed readout and the unresolved experiment. Shared program membership alone does not establish regulation between a target and a different proposed marker. No composite evidence score or target ranking was introduced.

*Within-stage programs and composition.* We kept all 117 fixed program memberships and weights and reused the signature-excluded scores from the selected genetic-identity clusters. The primary model was `program_score_z ~ continuum_z + factor(fibrosis_stage) + factor(inferred_sex)`, fitted separately in GSE162694 and GSE213621 for each of the two co-primary axes. These analyses used the disease units with complete data, 109 and 258 genetic-identity clusters, respectively (367 total), and every program has an explicit denominator and failure state. These cohorts had been examined before, so they provide replication evidence but not untouched prospective validation. The three source-overlap cohorts contribute descriptive profiles only.

The composition sensitivity added 15 orthonormal log-ratio coordinates from the 16 lineage fractions estimated with InstaPrism under the BayesPrism model [ref: BayesPrism] [ref: InstaPrism]. Fractions below 1e-6 were floored before taking logarithms, and a normalized Helmert basis supplied the coordinates. We report the frequency of floored fractions by lineage and cohort. Both models used the same complete genetic-identity clusters and outcome units. Axis scores were standardized within the complete cohort, and singular designs were marked untestable. We kept ordinary least-squares coefficients, standard errors and two-sided t tests, and exported HC3 standard errors and t-reference sensitivity tests separately [ref: HC3 estimator]. Fixed-effect inverse-variance summaries required both evaluation cohorts. BH correction covered all 117 programs separately in each cohort, axis and model family, and in each meta-analysis axis and model family, with untestable members counted in the family size. Support required both co-primary meta-analysis BH q values below 0.05 and consistent signs in all four cohort-by-axis fits. A separate, stricter field required BH q below 0.05 in each cohort and each axis. Neither criterion implies 117 independent biological processes. Composition-adjusted associations can still reflect imperfect deconvolution or shared expression measurements, and attenuation does not estimate how much cell composition contributes. We added no nonlinear test; the three-degree-of-freedom spline tests and their complete-family corrections are unchanged. We make no claim about program coherence from this analysis, because the reconstruction and calibration checks that such a claim requires were unresolved.

*Cell and tissue context.* Single-cell source-lineage labels are kept as discovery context. They do not show the cellular origin of a transcript after ambient RNA correction, and they do not independently confirm a bulk RNA result. Signal-level ATAC overlap and program-level ATAC association keep their own lineage, coverage, donor denominators and evidence states. Spatial assay coverage is reported for the complete program family. Formal matched-background tests are confined to the programs originally tested and their stated multiplicity family. Observable programs that were not tested do not receive a negative spatial result, and a program-level spatial result is never transferred to each member gene. Paired-biopsy program and gene analyses keep their within-person unit, complete testing families and measured changes. Repeat biopsies are not treated as technical replicates. This analysis does not establish within-stage, cell-specific genetic regulation.

*Protein readouts.* Adjusted liver DIA-MS disease-association results keep their log2 abundance contrast and full-proteome BH correction. They come from 58 source samples (12 controls and 46 with MASLD), adjusted for acquisition batch, age, BMI and sex. Earlier program-protein tests keep their original subset and identifiers and are reported separately; they are not mapped to all 117 program-level tests. We export complete member-protein observability without treating individual member-protein associations as a test of an entire RNA program.

*Display and reproducibility.* The integrated figure uses the nine overlapping descriptive windows. Its complete program map averages the five cohort window means equally; per-cohort means and participant counts are available. Window membership did not select genes, transitions, thresholds or significance. Native assay effects and their distinct error models remain separate, and repeated RNA-derived features do not count as independent modalities. All scripts set seed 20260906 and record R session information. Checks of allele swaps, ambiguity, posterior mass, complete families, participant denominators and reproduction of source effects accompany the output.

### GWAS fine-mapping

GWAS SuSiE fits used ancestry-specific LD [ref: SuSiE]. European analyses used the full PolyFun UK Biobank European LD reference with L = 10 [ref: PolyFun]. Non-European analyses used the ancestry-matched GWAS LD panel built from 1000 Genomes where one was available, while the eQTL side remained European. The fine-mapping unit was one converged SuSiE credible set, identified by study, trait, locus, ancestry and credible-set ID. Sets with raw PIP sums outside 0.90 to 1.10 failed the primary reliability criterion. Lead variants were clumped with PLINK 1.9 (r² 0.1 within 500 kb) [ref: PLINK]. European strata were clumped against 1000 Genomes European genotypes at p < 5 × 10⁻⁸. Non-European strata (MVP AFR, AMR and EAS, Biobank Japan, and Pan-UK Biobank AFR and CSA) were clumped against the ancestry-matched 1000 Genomes panel at p < 1 × 10⁻⁵, and each lead was labeled genome-wide significant (p < 5 × 10⁻⁸, or 3.24 × 10⁻⁸ for AFR) or suggestive. AFR leads with p between 3.24 × 10⁻⁸ and 5 × 10⁻⁸ are therefore labeled suggestive although they pass the conventional threshold. Of the 6,415 reliable credible sets, 6,227 come from non-European strata, and 2,363 of these lie at leads labeled suggestive (43 of 207 direct-trait sets and 2,320 of 6,208 enzyme sets). Each locus spanned the lead position ± 0.5 Mb in all 50 studies.

Fine-mapping used `susieR::susie_rss` on the LD matrix with 1 × 10⁻⁶ added to the diagonal. Quantitative traits used effect sizes and standard errors, with n equal to the total sample size. Binary traits used z scores, with n equal to the effective sample size 4/(1/N_cases + 1/N_controls). The number of effects, iteration limit and purity threshold were the susieR 0.14.2 defaults (L = 10, at most 100 iterations, minimum absolute within-set correlation 0.5). Credible-set coverage was 0.95, and the seed was set to 42 before each fit. Where SuSiE did not converge, PIPs and credible sets were set to missing and the locus contributes no credible set. Credible sets were counted only for main-portfolio tier-1 and tier-2 studies. The GWAS-side SuSiE fit inside the colocalization analysis was refit for each gene-by-GWAS pair on the GWAS variants at the positions of the gene's eQTL fit, with no p-value threshold, so it ran for every stratum whether or not that stratum had standalone fine-mapping. It required at least 100 GWAS-eQTL overlapping variants and at least 50 variants with LD at the eQTL-fit positions, used the ancestry-matched GWAS LD matrix with 1 × 10⁻³ added to the diagonal (1 × 10⁻⁶ in standalone fine-mapping), z scores with n equal to the effective sample size for binary traits and the total sample size for quantitative traits, L = 10, a minimum absolute within-set correlation of 0.5, at most 500 iterations and residual variance fixed at 1, and the seed was set to 42 before each fit.

Cross-ancestry fine-mapping within MVP used SuSiEx [version to be supplied] [ref: SuSiEx] and MESuSiE 1.0 [ref: MESuSiE]. Shared loci were defined per MVP trait where a lead with p < 5 × 10⁻⁸ in at least two MVP ancestries (EUR, AFR, AMR, EAS) lay within 500 kb, and the window spanned the contributing leads ± 100 kb. For ALT, AST and NAFLD this gave 81 loci. MESuSiE returned a fit at 79 of them, and SuSiEx returned at least one credible set at 77. SuSiEx computed LD from 1000 Genomes genotypes for all four ancestries and used credible-set coverage 0.95, minimum purity 0.5, MAF ≥ 0.005 and its multi-step choice of the number of effects, and it removed credible sets with no variant at marginal p < 1 × 10⁻⁵. MESuSiE used PolyFun UK Biobank LD for EUR and 1000 Genomes LD for the other ancestries with 1 × 10⁻³ added to the diagonal, L = 10, at most 200 iterations, residual variance not estimated, minimum absolute within-set correlation 0.5 and coverage 0.95, and arms with fewer than 50 variants were dropped.

### Liver-eQTL colocalization

GWAS strata were integrated with the Broadaway liver-eQTL meta-analysis of 1,183 donors. Single-variant approximate Bayes factor (ABF) colocalization, which uses no LD, was evaluated for every testable gene–GWAS pair [ref: coloc]. Multi-signal SuSiE colocalization was attempted only when four prerequisites were all available: a converged precomputed eQTL SuSiE fit, sufficient overlapping variants, a usable ancestry-specific GWAS LD matrix and a converged GWAS SuSiE fit, with at least one credible set in each fit [ref: coloc-SuSiE]. ABF and SuSiE colocalization used coloc 5.2.3 with priors p1 = p2 = 1 × 10⁻⁴ and p12 = 5 × 10⁻⁶. If any SuSiE prerequisite failed or was structurally absent, the pair received an explicit ABF-fallback or ABF-only state, which we did not interpret as a negative SuSiE result. The prespecified high-resolution support threshold was SuSiE PP.H4 > 0.5. Each colocalized gene took the trait class of the tier-1/2 GWAS with its highest SuSiE PP.H4. When PP.H4 was exactly equal in more than one GWAS, the GWAS whose study identifier sorts first alphabetically was used. MAU2 is the only gene whose tied maximum spans two trait classes: its PP.H4 is 1 in the MVP European ALT, AST and NAFLD GWAS, so it is counted under the enzyme class, and a rule that preferred direct traits would give 422 enzyme and 14 direct genes. Counts of support from any trait class do not use this rule. The prior p12 enters only the shared-variant hypothesis, so PP.H4 under another p12 follows exactly from the stored posteriors as k·PP.H4/(k·PP.H4 + 1 − PP.H4), where k is the new p12 divided by 5 × 10⁻⁶. At p12 = 1 × 10⁻⁶, 122 of the 436 supported genes fall to PP.H4 ≤ 0.5. In the historical named-r8 prior sensitivity, p12 = 1 × 10⁻⁵ moves 29 genes tested at PP.H4 ≤ 0.5 above threshold; that screen was not rerun under the adopted eligibility qualification (Supplementary Note 1). Prior sensitivities do not change the adopted classes. We also refit the 18 tier-1/2 GWAS fits with SuSiE PP.H4 of 1.0 that carry the only support of 14 colocalized genes, varying the LD diagonal, the residual variance and the variants fitted one at a time (Supplementary Note 1). The broader definition was SuSiE or ABF PP.H4 > 0.5. The evidence-class interface uses the SuSiE call. ### Accessible-chromatin QTL colocalization

We tested each European tier-1/2 GWAS signal that colocalized with a liver eQTL (SuSiE PP.H4 > 0.5; 581 study-lead signals) against bulk-liver chromatin accessibility QTL (caQTL) summary statistics from 138 donors (118 of European, 17 of African and 3 of admixed genetic similarity) [ref: Currin et al., 2025]. On the GWAS side, we refit the GWAS SuSiE model of the eQTL colocalization (PolyFun UK Biobank European LD, L = 10), reproduced the stored fits and took the log Bayes factor row of the colocalizing signal. For each accessible peak whose center lay within 500 kb of the GWAS lead, per-variant log Bayes factors were computed from the caQTL effect and its variance under a single-effect assumption (prior SD 0.15). GWAS positions were lifted to GRCh38 and matched on position and unordered alleles, and only SNVs with caQTL MAF ≥ 0.01 could match. Variants that could not be matched kept their posterior mass as unshared. The two full rows were compared with `coloc.bf_bf` (coloc 5.2.3; p1 = p2 = 1×10⁻⁴, p12 = 5×10⁻⁶, the priors of the eQTL colocalization). A signal entered when PP.H4 > 0.8 and the GWAS and caQTL lead variants had LD r² ≥ 0.5, the entry rule of Currin et al. A region was linked to a Gene Catalog gene only when, at the same peak, the caQTL row also colocalized (coloc.bf_bf PP.H4 > 0.8, same priors) with the eQTL SuSiE effect of that gene that had colocalized with the same GWAS lead variant. The eQTL fits store GRCh37 positions without alleles, so these positions were lifted to GRCh38 with the same chain file and matched to caQTL positions on position only; where a position held more than one caQTL variant, the variant with the smallest p was matched. This comparison used no lead-variant LD rule and links 108 genes. As a sensitivity analysis, we required LD r² ≥ 0.8 between the caQTL lead and the eQTL lead in the PolyFun UK Biobank European LD panel used for the eQTL fits. The caQTL lead was the matched variant with the smallest caQTL p, and the eQTL lead was the variant with the largest posterior in the colocalized eQTL effect. Of the 108 genes, 100 passed, and 107 passed at r² ≥ 0.5. The GWAS signal was matched across GWAS refits by its lead variant, so for 14 of the 108 genes every qualifying GWAS-caQTL entry comes from another gene's refit that shares the same GWAS lead; all 14 pass the LD rule. For two genes, NARS2 and MTMR3, the colocalized eQTL SuSiE effect has no marginal eQTL association in the Broadaway summary statistics: the smallest marginal p in the credible set is 0.18 for NARS2, and the one variant in the MTMR3 set has p = 0.096. Both pass the LD rule (r² 0.904 and 0.989), so the rule does not remove them. eQTL and caQTL donors may overlap, so this three-way support is not independent.

The unit of description is the GWAS signal, and the unit of inference for the comparison is the independent locus (leads merged within 500 kb). One prespecified test (family of one, no correction) compared liver-only loci with platelet-count-only loci by logistic regression. Platelet-count loci came from the MVP European platelet GWAS. Each arm excluded loci with a lead from the other arm within 500 kb. Chromosomes 1 and 19 were excluded because a pilot had examined them. The model was adjusted for −log10 lead p, the number of genome-wide significant variants within 500 kb, the number of testable peaks and −log10 minimum caQTL p. Stop rules required reproduction of the GWAS fits, positive controls at known loci, at least 120 liver-only and 60 platelet-only testable loci, and a rate of 0.10 or below in a negative control that circularly shifts the caQTL values among shared variants. All passed. The negative-control rates were 0.0407 and 0.0513 without the LD entry rule and 0.0233 and 0.0256 with it. Without the LD rule, the prediction for that negative control, 0.03 or below per arm, was missed. The positive controls on chromosomes 1 and 19 had been examined in the pilot and were not blind; the chromosome 5 controls were blind. Locus proportions carry Wilson intervals, and the descriptive lead-level difference used 1,000 locus-cluster bootstrap resamples (seed 20260923). Fits used susieR 0.14.2.

### Joint testing universe and evidence classes

The native bulk and colocalization interface contains 14,704 stable Ensembl identities with a disease-versus-control bulk test and a tier-1/2 liver-eQTL record; a finite SuSiE result is not required for this interface. Genetic and RNA support, qualified by applicability, define the classes; protein, spatial and single-cell endpoints do not. Figures 1B and 3G use the 3,589 evaluated genes retained after the shared-posterior check of carried negatives under the adopted priority rule: a positive eligible result takes precedence, then any untestable primary study, then a finite result below threshold, then no SuSiE result. The remaining native-interface genes comprise 10,928 with no multi-signal result and 187 priority-untestable genes. These are excluded from the evaluated comparison rather than counted as negatives. The test definition was specified before the original analysis; adopting the eligibility qualification changes the historical 3,769-gene universe. Supplementary Note 3 retains that historical result and the 3,736-gene alternative any-eligible-study sensitivity. Expected overlap is stratified by 10 AveExpr quantile bins (ties broken by Ensembl ID), and the exact null is the convolution of the per-bin hypergeometric distributions. The two-sided p sums the null probability of every total x with |x − E| ≥ |O − E|. The effect measure is the Mantel-Haenszel OR with its Robins-Breslow-Greenland 95% CI [ref: Mantel-Haenszel; Robins-Breslow-Greenland]. Historical expression-matching diagnostics, including 13 disease-state genes against 21.3 expected and an unstratified power range of OR 0.46 to 1.77, were computed in the earlier 3,769-gene universe and were not rerun after qualification. They are not substituted for diagnostics of the adopted universe.

The symbol-grouped comparison before identity adjudication contained 14,720 rows, a distinct denominator from the native stable-ID interface. After 31 jointly eligible annotation quarantines and 187 priority-untestable identities, the adopted Gene Catalog has 14,502 jointly eligible identities; the historical r8 joint class universe had 14,682. The Catalog keeps one record per adjudicated identity in the union of bulk and tier-1/2 colocalization records. Absent or untestable assay comparisons produce an untested class. Three groups have no record and are not counted: identities quarantined during adjudication (a symbol mapping to multiple Ensembl IDs or an ID absent from GENCODE v49), second Ensembl IDs of a symbol already kept under another ID, and program-member genes outside both assay universes. Six colocalization IDs absent from GENCODE v49 are instead carried on the record keyed to the same gene’s bulk Ensembl ID [exact reconciliation in supplementary table; number to be assigned]. One canonical RNA call, ENSG00000278732 (Metazoa_SRP, a family symbol carried by eight bulk IDs), is quarantined. The Catalog therefore retains 1,853 of the unchanged 1,854 bulk calls. The November 6 eligibility release changes 188 genetic states and 180 classes (169 unresolved and 11 established-state-associated genes become untested); eight changed genetic states were already in the untested class. Named r8/r9 files remain historical, and all RNA calls, effects and directions and all positive genetic memberships are retained.

Evidence classes summarize support categories; they are not powered tests of genetic absence. A missing SuSiE result or a partially untestable gene is not evidence of genetic absence, and assay states are kept separately from the support labels. Classes were defined as follows.

- Genetically anchored (labeled Genetics only in Figure 3G): SuSiE PP.H4 > 0.5 and not a disease-versus-control call.
- Established-state associated (labeled Transcriptomics only in Figure 3G): a disease-versus-control call and no SuSiE PP.H4 > 0.5.
- Concordant, supported on both axes (labeled Both in Figure 3G): both SuSiE PP.H4 > 0.5 and a disease-versus-control call. The direction of the genetic effect is not compared with the RNA direction, because the Catalog genetics rows carry no direction.
- Unresolved (labeled Neither in Figure 3G): jointly testable and meeting neither threshold.
- Untested (not shown as a class in Figure 3G): absent from an assay universe, or untestable in one domain. Quarantined identities have no Catalog record and are not counted in any class.

Sensitivity classes substituted the SuSiE-or-ABF union for SuSiE. Genetic trait scope was assigned from the primary SuSiE result as direct disease, enzyme, both or none, and genetic confidence was assigned as SuSiE, ABF-only or none. A broader prioritized gene universe and alternative transcriptomic contrasts were analyzed as sensitivities and are not substituted into main claims.

### Regulatory context of colocalized signals

For each colocalized gene–study pair, we selected one primary signal pair deterministically and intersected its normalized SNP.PP.H4 posterior probability with open chromatin from two liver snATAC sources across five shared lineages. Direct MASLD/liver-fat and liver-enzyme traits were kept separate. Counts use the gene as the unit, taking the best state over a gene's study pairs. Overlap below 5% of the posterior in both sources is grouped with absent overlap. The locus-matched expectation for a pair and lineage is the fraction of its mapped tested variants inside a peak of that lineage in either source. Observed and expected masses were averaged per gene and compared by 2,000 gene bootstrap resamples (seed 20260923). This expectation matches the window, its variant density and its peak coverage, but not LD or allele frequency. Partial or absent peak overlap is unresolved context, not a negative functional test.

### Hotspot program discovery

The 117 Hotspot programs were discovered in the single-cell atlas [ref: Hotspot]. Their memberships, weights and names were fixed at discovery and were not changed or rediscovered by any analysis described here. Discovery kept a library's hepatocytes, cholangiocytes, fibroblasts or macrophages only when that library had at least 30 cells of the lineage, and kept T cells without a minimum. The program-stage models below score these same retained cells. Discovery ran separately in each lineage: hepatocytes (657,412 cells), fibroblasts (38,439), macrophages (19,595), cholangiocytes (32,402) and T cells (42,295). The fibroblast, macrophage, cholangiocyte and T-cell runs excluded GSE136103 and GSE192742. The hepatocyte run included every dataset.

For hepatocytes, fibroblasts, macrophages and cholangiocytes, Hotspot used its `danb` model on raw counts, with each cell's summed counts over the genes kept by the biotype, chromosome, gene-list and 1% detection filters below as the depth term. For T cells, the input matrix held log-normalized values (log1p of counts per 10,000), and the `danb` model was applied to those values instead of the integer counts stored in the same file, with the per-cell sum of the log values over the same genes as the depth term. The latent space was a 30-component PCA of library-size-normalized, log-transformed counts for hepatocytes, fibroblasts, macrophages and cholangiocytes, and the atlas Harmony components for T cells. The neighbor graph was an unweighted 30-nearest-neighbor graph (50 for macrophages). Genes were restricted to protein-coding genes and lncRNAs. We removed genes on chrY and chrM, ribosomal (RPS, RPL, MRPS, MRPL), mitochondrial (MT-) and hemoglobin genes and a list of sex-chromosome genes; the four non-T-cell inputs also had a list of X-chromosome escape genes removed. Genes detected in fewer than 1% of the lineage's cells were dropped. Local correlations were computed for the 2,000 genes with the highest autocorrelation Z among genes with autocorrelation FDR < 0.05 and Z ≥ 7. Modules were called with `create_modules` (at least 20 genes, core genes only, FDR < 0.05), and each gene's weight is its mean local-correlation Z with the members of its module. This gave 30 hepatocyte, 29 fibroblast, 19 macrophage, 28 cholangiocyte and 11 T-cell programs (117 in total). A program's name records the cell-type subset it was discovered in.

Content lineage was assigned separately by scoring each program's fixed member genes against donor-level per-lineage pseudobulk. Scores were z-scored across lineages, so that the index reads specificity rather than abundance. The index was validated against donor-paired same-atlas contrasts before use. Content lineage is an added annotation and does not change program memberships, weights or names.

### Single-cell program stage models

The primary ordinal models use at most 59 source-qualified donors whose datasets could be ambient-corrected (GSE189600 excluded; GSE202379 P98 excluded; P30 retained as a qualified reference with an exclusion sensitivity). Each program score was modeled as `score ~ stage + dataset` by generalized least squares with a separate residual variance per dataset (REML; `nlme::gls` with `varIdent`; Pinheiro et al.). We used this model because the residual standard deviation differed between GSE244832 and GSE202379: the ratio is 3.8 for Ductular injury and 3.0 for Stromal ECM (variance ratios about 14 and 9), with a median of 1.6 and a range of 0.07 to 16.4 across the 117 programs. Donors in the two single-stage datasets (GSE174748 and GSE185477, Healthy only) carry no stage information and are not fitted. The p value is (1 + the number of 19,999 within-dataset stage permutations, seed 20260923, with |t| at least the observed value) / 20,000. BH correction is applied over all 117 programs, and selection also requires a stability median of at least 0.5. Stability is the median top-50-gene Jaccard over left-out datasets, so a program whose genes are defined by one dataset can still pass. Without GSE244832, the Jaccard is 0.026 for Stromal ECM and 0.000 for Ductular injury, against 0.31 to 1.0 for other left-out datasets; 56 of 117 programs fall below 0.2 and 40 below 0.1. Stromal ECM was discovered in hepatocyte nuclei without doublet removal. In simulation, the model-based 95% interval covered the true effect in 94.2% (true beta 0) and 95.5% (true beta 0.30) of draws. The equal-variance OLS fit (at most 61 source records, including two GSE189600 records of unresolved biological independence), with BH over 117 programs and an HC3 sensitivity, is a supplementary sensitivity (Supplementary Note 8). The per-dataset-variance model was specified after the per-dataset slopes of Ductular injury and Stromal ECM, an inverse-variance combination of those slopes and the equal-variance results had been seen, and before it was fitted to any program; those values made its result for these two programs largely predictable. It changes which dataset carries the estimate: in this model GSE202379 carries 94% (Ductular injury) and 91% (Stromal ECM) of the information about the stage slope, whereas in an equal-variance fit of the same donors GSE244832 supplies 84% and 94% of the slope numerator.

Using the corrected donor labels, the first 2,000 complete null draws gave at least one 117-program BH call in 4.85% of draws. Known-truth simulation retained 2,000 draws for each of beta 0 and beta 0.30; model-based 95% coverage was 94.4% and 94.25%, respectively, with no failed fits. Detection experiments retain the previously fixed alternatives, real scores, 1,000 within-dataset draws and the permutation threshold at 0.05/117. They measure conditional sensitivity, not absence of effects. Seeds are 20260923. The two focal programs were selected in an earlier analysis and were not reselected after correction.

Primary-model sensitivities average stored scores over QC-passing cells or exclude donors with fewer than 20 T cells. Each preserves the 117-program BH family; the T-cell check changes only the 11 T-cell permutation p values. The corrected equal-variance sensitivity includes 61 source records, including two GSE189600 records of unresolved independence. Historical gate-specific significance claims are not carried forward. Projected scores are not native Hotspot units.

### Single-nucleus ATAC-seq

The snATAC analyses used the two liver sources listed under Data availability (GSE244832 and GSE281367), which supplied lineage-resolved open chromatin [ref: GSE244832 and GSE281367 source studies]. In GSE244832, lineage labels were transferred from the single-nucleus RNA atlas using the existing sample correspondences. Those correspondences are not independently verified cross-assay donor matches. ATAC gene-activity scores (normalized to 10,000 and log-transformed) were projected into the assigned RNA reference's principal-component space (3,000 highly variable genes, 30 components), and each nucleus took the majority CellTypist label of its ten nearest RNA neighbors. In GSE281367, each nucleus received the label with the highest marker gene-activity score (summed activity of the marker genes divided by total activity) among ten marker sets. Labels were mapped to five lineages: hepatocyte, stellate (CellTypist fibroblasts in GSE244832), macrophage (including Kupffer cells in GSE281367), cholangiocyte and T/NK.

Peaks were called with MACS3 through SnapATAC2 in each cohort without condition labels (narrow peaks, shift −100, extension 200 bp, hg38 ENCODE blacklist v2 removed) [ref: SnapATAC2] [ref: MACS3]. A pooled lineage peak (q < 0.05) was kept when it overlapped a donor-level peak (q < 0.10; donor-lineage groups with at least 20 nuclei) in at least two donors. For each lineage, the peaks of the two cohorts were merged into fixed 500-bp intervals with SnapATAC2 `merge_peaks`, and intervals overlapping the blacklist were removed. Each count is one deduplicated fragment record overlapping the peak, summed in donor-by-lineage pseudobulks.

Differential accessibility compared MASH with normal donors that had at least 20 nuclei in the lineage. Peaks passed `edgeR::filterByExpr` with an intercept-only design in each cohort and were kept when testable in both cohorts. We then used TMM normalization, `limma::voomWithQualityWeights`, `lmFit` and robust `eBayes` with the design `~ group`, with BH correction within each lineage and cohort. A peakwise two-cohort candidate required q < 0.05 in both cohorts with the same sign. Jointly testable peaks numbered 30,650 in hepatocytes and 9,264 in stellate cells, and no macrophage peak was jointly testable. With 5/9 and 6/6 normal/MASH donors per cohort, 38,066 of 39,914 jointly testable peaks are indeterminate and 1,844 are source-dependent (q < 0.05 in one cohort only; 1,505 share sign with the other cohort).

Directional replication was evaluated in a separate BH family of three lineages, retaining the untestable macrophage comparison in that family. Peakwise two-cohort calls, this lineage-level test, program tests and motif tests have separate hypothesis families. Sensitivities compared robust edgeR quasi-likelihood with the primary voom analysis, using identical fragment counts, donors, peaks and designs, and separately examined donor sums of binarized nucleus accessibility. These effect units were kept distinct. Recorded-batch models used the same donors and peaks as condition-only models. We reported condition-by-batch overlap, design rank, residual degrees of freedom and leave-one-donor-out effects; age, sex and BMI were not estimable from the supplied ATAC metadata. Fitted-negative-binomial null simulations were model diagnostics, not biological FDR estimates. Negative-binomial simulations of bounded binary counts were not used to select a method (Supplementary Figure S5AA).

### Descriptive accessibility links and held-donor prediction

Distance and Cicero links were learned separately in the two disease cohorts for hepatocytes and stellate cells, without disease-label selection. Networks used seeded, donor-confined aggregates of 50 nuclei, at least 100 and at most 1,000 nuclei per donor and equal donor weighting. Harmonized candidates lay within ±250 kb of the gene TSS; promoters used strand-aware ±2-kb windows. Promoter-overlapping predictors were excluded. Disease annotation retained at most ten ranked positive Cicero links per gene; continuous coaccessibility scores are neither probabilities nor p values. Cross-cohort overlap used only peak–gene pairs observable in both cohorts. The shared consensus peaks qualify independence of feature ascertainment. Links annotate the fixed 117 programs and original weights without redefining their membership, testing new programs, or transferring a peak q value to a gene. Motif-family compatibility does not identify TF activity or binding.

Prediction used genuine paired GSE296875 hepatocytes, 39 donors, with the original five outer and three inner donor folds. Promoter-only, promoter-plus-distance and promoter-plus-Cicero models used the same ridge readout, normalization, donor weighting and at most ten distal predictors per gene. Unlike positive-only disease annotation maps, the fixed predictive comparison retained its original signed-nonzero Cicero selection. The wider regularization grid was fixed at 0.1, 1, 10, …, 100,000,000 and tuned within training folds for every model; original-grid results remained separate. Preprocessing, network learning and feature selection used training donors only. Scores were compared on identical eligible gene–donor–fold keys, averaging gene/aggregate errors within each donor before comparing methods; excluded genes and tuning at the largest penalty were recorded.

The bounded robustness set comprised aggregation seeds 43 and 44 on the original capped cell roster; a 500-nucleus donor cap with seed 42; within-donor/lineage RNA pairing shuffles with seeds 43–45 before aggregation; and training-only omission of donors 342, 786 and 470, chosen by largest absolute influence in the existing Cicero-versus-distance scores, ties by donor ID. Held-out targets and both fold assignments were retained. Networks were refitted for each changed training population; three unaffected omission folds reused exact baseline predictions. Pairing shuffles preserve donor RNA marginals and do not remove all donor-level chromatin–RNA association. Intervals used 2,000 seeded paired donor bootstraps conditional on fitted predictions, without model-refitting uncertainty. Original and post hoc Holm families were preserved; new stress intervals are descriptive, with no new significance search. Robust internal predictive support required gains over both baselines in every unshuffled scenario. It does not establish independent MASLD validation or edge-level correctness.

Runs belonging to one physical library were counted jointly for barcode correction and UMI deduplication; independent captures stayed separate. ### Spatial transcriptomic analyses

We integrated spatial organization, zonation and cross-cohort annotations while keeping each source's measured-gene background, biological unit and provenance. None of the spatial analyses supplies a valid MASLD-versus-healthy disease-effect estimand. GSE192741 and Vu et al. (2026) test residual spatial autocorrelation against matched genes. Yakubovsky et al. (2026) is a non-MASLD tissue-context analysis. GSE287826, a NanoString GeoMx series, was not analyzed, because its gene axis was not loaded and no public key joins its areas of interest to donors. Spatial results therefore localize fixed genes and prespecified programs in tissue; we do not describe them as independent disease-associated change.

Visium inputs were the source-processed Space Ranger outputs, without realignment: five human GSE192741 sections (mouse sections were not used) and ten Vu et al. CytAssist FFPE arrays. Per Guilliams et al. Table S6, the four GSE192741 donors (H35–H38) were sampled at gastric bypass (H35, H37), cholecystectomy (H38) or resection of colorectal-cancer liver metastases (H36, who reported six standard alcohol units daily), with recorded steatosis of 35–40%, none, 70% and 10% and fibrosis of none, mild, clear and mild. H37 and H38 also contributed single-nucleus libraries to the atlas. Spots were kept with at least 200 detected genes, at least 500 counts and at most 20% mitochondrial counts, and genes detected in at least ten spots were kept. Cell-type abundance per spot was estimated with cell2location [ref: cell2location]. The reference was a negative binomial regression model trained on raw counts of the CellTypist-annotated single-cell atlas, with the 16 cell types as labels and dataset as batch. The two Visium sources used different reference builds. For GSE192741, the reference came from an earlier build of the atlas (1,170,457 cells from six datasets), subsampled to 150,000 cells (seed 42). Its training log does not name the six datasets. The current atlas has 61,861 more cells (1,232,318), exactly the number of GSE136103 cells, so GSE136103 was probably the dataset left out. For Vu et al., the reference came from a later build that includes all seven datasets, with at most 9,375 cells per cell type (115,562 cells; seed 42). Training used genes detected in at least ten cells, 250 epochs, batch size 2,500, learning rate 0.002 and 1,000 posterior samples. The spatial model used the genes shared with the reference and detected in at least five spots (10,738 for GSE192741 and 10,056 for Vu et al.), section or array as batch, 15 expected cells per spot and detection alpha 20. It trained for 30,000 epochs on the 6,546 GSE192741 spots and 15,000 on Vu et al., with 1,000 posterior samples. Downstream analyses use the 5% posterior quantile of abundance (q05).

Each Visium spot received a program score: the z-scored expression of its measured fixed member genes, weighted by their original L1 weights rescaled to sum to one. The prespecified confirmatory design residualizes the score for the cell2location hepatocyte abundance (the discovery lineage of both programs), log1p library size and log1p detected genes. A model residualized for all 16 cell2location q05 abundance factors and the same two covariates was added afterwards as a supplementary falsification test of composition dependence; the former Figure 5F (now supplementary) plots it. Residual Moran's I on six-neighbor spot graphs built within connected tissue islands [ref: Moran's I] was compared with 9,999 random gene sets scored and residualized identically (seed 42 for GSE192741, 1042 for Vu). Random sets were matched on expression, detection rate, biotype, mitochondrial and ribosomal status and correlation with the hepatocyte abundance. Expression, detection and correlation bins were relaxed by up to two bins when fewer than ten candidate genes matched; in the four former Figure 5F runs this applied to one Vu et al. Stromal ECM gene (C7), whose expression and detection bins were relaxed by one. Control genes were drawn independently of one another, so null sets do not reproduce the gene-gene co-expression of a fixed program. Both programs score highest outside hepatocytes (Stromal ECM in fibroblasts and Ductular injury in cholangiocytes), and matching did not use correlation with the abundance of those lineages. The null therefore asks whether a program score is more autocorrelated than a set of independently drawn genes with matched per-gene properties, not whether it exceeds other co-expressed programs. The reported excess is the observed statistic minus the null mean. p = (k + 1)/(9,999 + 1), where k counts draws at or above the observed value, so the smallest attainable p is 1e-4. BH correction covers the two prespecified programs within each source; no prespecified test compares the two programs. Two descriptive analyses with the 16-abundance model were added after the primary result and were not prespecified. First, the matched-gene null was applied to every fixed program with at least eight measured genes and at least 20% of its L1 weight retained (114 of 117 in each source). Control genes for each program were matched on correlation with the abundance of its discovery lineage, genes of every fixed program were excluded from the control pool, each program had 9,999 draws from one random stream per source (seeds 42 and 1042), and BH correction covered the 114 programs within each source. Second, the two prespecified programs were rerun with control genes matched on correlation with the abundance of the lineage in which each scores highest (fibroblasts for Stromal ECM, cholangiocytes for Ductular injury), with BH over the two programs. The null resamples genes and holds donors and arrays fixed. Donor-first estimates are used where a donor key exists (five GSE192741 sections collapse to four donors). An exact sign-flip test of per-donor excess has a smallest attainable p of 0.0625 with four donors and is reported descriptively. Array-level results without a donor key are reported as source-dependent and never as donor-level inference. Positive spatial autocorrelation without matched calibration does not establish program-specific tissue architecture.

### Xenium cell-type location

Figure 5C uses the Xenium in situ data of Tzouanas et al. (2026) [ref: Tzouanas et al. 2026] from the Single Cell Portal (SCP3359): 18 donors with one tissue piece each (9 controls and 9 MASH-cirrhosis explants), 723,466 cells and a 477-gene panel. Raw counts, cell labels, donors and cell centroids (µm) are the source's. The analysis plan was written and checksummed before expression values were loaded (PRESPEC.md in the X1 analysis directory). Two label changes were made after a first run failed two prespecified positive checks and are recorded with it: the source's `Stellate.DNASE1L3+CD36+` cells (DNASE1L3, FCN2 and LYVE1 high, PDGFRB low) were treated as liver sinusoidal endothelial cells, and `VSMC.MYH11+` cells as vascular smooth muscle. The unit is the donor. For each donor and cell type, the level of a gene is its mean raw count per cell. Hepatocytes use interior cells whose six nearest neighbors are all hepatocytes. For each non-hepatocyte type, interior cells have no hepatocyte centroid within 15 µm. A type uses interior-cell means only when at least seven donors in each arm have at least 30 such cells; under that rule, a donor with fewer than 30 uses all cells of that type. If the type-level seven-per-arm requirement fails, all donors use all cells of that type. Hepatocyte spillover is estimated from three hepatocyte-restricted genes (HPX, KNG1, FBP1): for each non-hepatocyte cell set, the leak is the median over the three genes of their mean in the set divided by their mean in interior hepatocytes, and the corrected level is the observed level minus the leak times the interior-hepatocyte level, floored at zero. Levels are averaged over the 9 control donors with equal weight. For display, the 12 cell types are merged into 7 columns by taking the highest member type, and each gene is scaled to its top column. A colocalized gene is shown when it is detected (at least one count) in at least 5% of its top cell type's cells in at least 7 of 9 controls and, for a non-hepatocyte type, its level is at least three times the expected spillover; 11 of 12 pass. The snRNA lineage of a gene is the atlas lineage with the highest mean CPM over lineage pseudobulk columns with at least 100,000 counts; these columns are sequencing runs, not verified donors. Counts per cell are not corrected for cell size. The panel is descriptive. The planned MASH-versus-control analysis compares corrected levels inside each gene's selected carrier lineage. A post hoc statistical correction repeats arm-dependent cell-set eligibility, spillover correction under that choice, and the control-defined carrier selection in each of all 48,620 donor-label assignments. Two-sided exact p values compare the absolute bounded contrast (case mean minus control mean)/(case mean plus control mean), which preserves the ordering of absolute log ratios for positive means; one-sided zeros are endpoints and two zero means give zero. No assignment is dropped. The 10,000 within-arm donor bootstraps (seed 20261027) repeat the same choices; empirical 2.5th/97.5th log-ratio quantiles retain infinite limits. These are pointwise bootstrap intervals, not simultaneous or selective intervals. Holm correction retains all 14 protein-supported and all 12 colocalized genes in separate families; the supplementary 103-gene family uses BH. Label exchangeability remains an assumption: the analysis does not remove age or tissue-source confounding between controls and the older cirrhosis explants (median age 38.5 versus 59.2 years). The disease comparison is therefore reported as exploratory text, separate from the descriptive healthy-liver panel.

### MASLD Gene Catalog construction

Gene Catalog entries were built only from checksummed output files of the analyses described above. Each gene or program keeps a stable identifier, evidence class, testing universe, assay-specific call, source dataset, relationship to the source publication and the graph of parent files. The evidence rows carry the native estimate and, for RNA, the BH q, but no standard error, interval, p value or unit count. The gene table adds, for RNA, the limma standard error, moderated t statistic and p value, and the unit counts of the fit (774 genetic-identity clusters: 138 control, 636 disease). PP.H4 is a posterior probability and has no standard error. Membership in a program is not expanded into gene-level protein, spatial, chromatin or functional evidence.

For a given assay, a gene, program, peak or variant receives one of seven evidence states: supported, discordant, tested negative, indeterminate, untestable, not applicable or source-dependent. A blank or non-significant comparison may reflect absent assay coverage, insufficient expression, weak molecular-QTL power, phenotype mismatch, an unavailable donor key, reuse of a source or a mechanism outside the resolution of the assay. A tested-negative state is reserved for an adequately observed, applicable and properly joined assay that fails a prespecified adequate-negative or equivalence rule. Non-significance, inadequate coverage, a failed source check or an assay that cannot observe the proposed mechanism remains indeterminate or untestable. The Gene Catalog defines no such rule for either domain, so no gene can be tested negative. A gene outside the eligible participant or measurement universe of a contrast is untestable for that contrast, not a negative result. The states describe the role of the evidence. They do not assign cause, give a probability that a target is valid, or rank genes on the assumption that agreement across modalities is best.

### Follow-up experiment routes

Next-experiment routes are deterministic functions of the evidence, molecular object and testability fields. A noncoding regulatory element routes to allele-aware reporter assays or locus editing in the lineage where the element is accessible. A disease-associated lncRNA without a genetic anchor routes to RNA depletion with readouts of neighboring genes and phenotype. A colocalized lncRNA or ambiguous lncRNA locus routes to paired RNA depletion and locus-level CRISPRi or allele perturbation, so that effects of the RNA product, of transcription and of the DNA element can be distinguished. No current record is a regulatory element, so the regulatory-element route is assigned to none. Of the 14,176 protein-coding molecular-object records, 13,997 receive gene perturbation with rescue and 179 newly untested records instead require the missing decisive assay. One newly untested lncRNA record also changes from RNA-product perturbation to the missing-assay route. Molecular-object route totals are 13,756 missing-assay, 13,997 protein-state perturbation, 412 RNA-product perturbation and 68 locus disambiguation. These are distinct from the five gene-class routes; activation, protein-level, lineage-restricted and context-specific designs are not assigned separately. The routing does not select new scientific results and does not use downstream outcomes. The signed Catalog fields are provisional, because the bulk, stage, composition and colocalization results they combine were not all computed on the same version of the data.

### Held-out replication in SteatoSITE

SteatoSITE (ENA PRJEB58625; Kendall et al., 2023) provides paired-end 2 x 50 bp total RNA (SMARTer Stranded Total RNA-Seq Pico v2) from archival FFPE liver, and the ENA sample records give NASH-CRN fibrosis stage codes 1 to 4. No code for F0 exists, so code 1 is read as F0–F1 and unstaged samples are not used as controls. Permission-only clinical data were not used. The families, models, controls and pass rules were fixed and hashed before any SteatoSITE expression was joined to stage. Reads were aligned with STAR 2.7.11b to GENCODE v49 and counted with the release fragment counter (`-s 2`). Read 1 and read 2 labels were swapped in some deposited runs, so mate order was determined per run from the data before counting. Of 658 samples, 652 libraries were counted. Quality control blinded to stage removed 114 libraries on technical metrics, 18 as possibly mixed samples by genotype, and 5 as the lower-depth library of a same-individual pair (KING kinship ≥ 0.354 within SteatoSITE; no SteatoSITE library matched a library of the five discovery cohorts), leaving 515 donors, 493 of them staged. Sex was inferred from XIST and Y-chromosome genes.

Models used limma `voomWithQualityWeights` with filterByExpr (grouped by stage) and RLE factors, `~ batch + inferred_sex + stage`, with nine sequencing batches. The primary family (two tests, Holm) took the slope b of SteatoSITE log2FC on discovery log2FC for F3–F4 and F2 versus F0–F1, over discovery BH calls kept in SteatoSITE. Discovery was the fixed 371-unit disease-only three-cohort grouped fit (see Grouped-stage comparison in GSE213621). P values are one-sided against 1,000 permutations of stage within batch x sex strata (seed 20260923); intervals come from 200 bootstrap draws within stage x sex (seed 20261007). A contrast was testable if 2.80 times the null SD was at most 0.30. The adjacent steps of Figure 3E formed a second Holm family of three, with discovery from the exact-stage fits. Positive controls were COL1A1, EPCAM and LUM up and GNMT and CYP2C19 down. The negative controls were the slope of the SteatoSITE sex coefficient on the discovery effects, the permutation null mean and a refit with RNA-quality covariates. Per-gene Catalog states used BH within kept genes and a per-gene minimum detectable effect. The within-cohort overlap analysis split the 493 donors into five random groups 50 times (seed 20261027), fitted F3–F4 versus F0–F1 in each group with the same model, and counted genes called (BH q < 0.05) in one or several groups. Genotype calls at PNPLA3 rs738409 failed Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (p = 8 x 10^-8), so prespecified genotype analyses were not run.

Three exploratory analyses were prespecified and hashed separately. Exon-intron split: intronic features were each release gene's span minus all exons of any gene on the same strand; genes whose remaining region overlapped another same-strand gene were removed, and intervals under 50 bp were dropped (15,266 genes kept). Intronic fragments were counted with featureCounts (`-s 2 -p --countReadPairs -B`, unique alignments) in the 493 staged libraries. Genes with at least 10 intronic reads in at least 50% of donors and, separately, at least 10 exonic reads in at least 50% of donors were kept (12,010). Exonic and intronic F3–F4 versus F0–F1 changes came from separate `voomWithQualityWeights` fits with the model above and TMM factors computed within each count type over the 493 libraries. The change in exon/intron ratio (Gaidatzis et al., 2015) was tested per gene with limma on each donor's log2 exonic minus log2 intronic CPM (0.5 prior; `~ batch + inferred_sex + stage group`; empirical Bayes with an abundance trend), BH over the 12,010 genes. The November 6 correction includes batch and sex in both the observed ratio model and all 200 stage permutations within batch x sex strata (seed 20261103); it reproduces the previously saved adjusted sensitivity to numerical precision. The global call-count p value uses (1 + null counts at least observed)/(200 + 1). Identity and shuffled-label checks verify that nuisance-stratum margins are preserved and the null actually changes fitted effects. Stage-only effects and calls remain explicitly historical. The limma implementation replaced a prespecified edgeR fit with donor terms, which was too slow to permute; the historical stage-only 300-gene comparison gave effect correlation r = 0.94, not evidence of equivalent inference. A nonsignificant ratio test is classified as unresolved and never as evidence that RNA processing or stability is unchanged. NMF axes: the fixed k6 bases were applied with the release transport (pairwise TMM to the stored reference, log2 CPM, cohort shift, row minimum, clipping, non-negative least squares). The same code reproduced the discovery scores (maximum difference 5 x 10^-16). Each SteatoSITE batch was shifted by its mean log2 CPM minus the discovery mean; scores without the shift were nearly identical (within-batch Spearman ≥ 0.993). Axis scores were standardized over the 493 staged donors and fitted with `~ batch + inferred_sex + stage`. The F3–F4 versus F0–F1 activity was tested one-sided in the discovery direction against 1,000 permutations of stage within batch x sex (seed 20261104), Holm over five axes. Liver eQTL direction: at expressed SNPs that are GTEx v8 liver significant variants for the same gene, one site per gene was chosen without allele fractions, and genes with at least 30 heterozygous donors (at least 10 reads, ALT fraction 0.1 to 0.9) were tested for agreement between the sign of the bin-centred ALT read share and the GTEx slope against 10,000 random sign assignments (seed 20261102).

### Gene Catalog annotation fields

Each field below was specified, with its controls and stop rules, before its outcome was computed, and its states were assigned in code by the prespecified reading rule. The seed was 20260923. Class-dependent counts use the Catalog evidence classes, the colocalization tables and the 774-unit bulk fit. No field enters an evidence class or a next-experiment route. The coding-variant, caQTL and postmortem-fat fields, and the composition sensitivity that added no field, are described in their own subsections above.

*Healthy-liver zonation.* For each Catalog gene, we computed mRNA position along the central-portal axis from the Yakubovsky et al. (2026) Visium deposit in five non-steatotic live donors (M4 to M8; unit the donor). We used spots in the source's zones 1 (central) to 8 (portal) and divided expression by spot total. Position was the center of mass of the zone-mean profile minus 4.5, taken as the median over the five donors. A gene was called central or portal only if it was detected in at least 5% of spots in every donor, fell on the same side in all five donors and passed a detection-decile threshold set from a zone-label permutation null (200 permutations). Genes below the detection rule are described as not measurable in Visium, not as unzonated. Hepatocyte protein zonation was copied from Weiss et al. (2026) (14 healthy individuals, Supplementary Table 3) and was not refitted.

The one prespecified test was the Spearman correlation between mRNA position and the Weiss coefficient over the 1,337 Catalog genes measured in both layers, excluding the landmark genes that define the zone axis. It used a two-sided gene-permutation p (10,000 permutations; family of one, alpha 0.05) and a 2,000-draw gene-bootstrap interval. A combined layer flag required p below 0.05, rho of at least 0.30 and a positive per-donor correlation in each of the five donors. Because genes covary, the permutation p is too small, so the donor condition and the rho floor govern the reading. The seed was numpy SeedSequence(20260923) with four streams. Controls: zone orientation was correct in 5 of 5 donors; 19 of 22 robust landmark genes had the same sign in both layers (rule at least 15); and 1.5% of zone-label permutations gave |rho| of 0.08 or more (rule at most 10%). No stop rule applied. Across the 1,337 genes measured in both layers, mRNA and protein positions agreed in direction (Spearman rho 0.39, 95% interval 0.34 to 0.44; positive in each of five donors). Weiss et al. reported a moderate RNA-protein zonation correlation against single-nucleus RNA (their Supplementary Fig. 9). This annotation describes healthy liver only and gives no information about zonation in MASLD.

*Assay observability.* Protein detection was taken from the PXD051911 proteomes: liver, 58 participants; plasma, 184 samples (143 baseline and 41 follow-up) from 143 participants. A gene was detected if its protein was observed in at least one sample. The field also records detection in at least half of the participants, using baseline samples only for plasma. CosMx targeting was read from the panel header of the GSE312698 CosMx data (Boesch et al., 2026): 942 Catalog genes have a single-gene probe, and 40 are covered by 19 probes shared between genes. Single-cell detection used donor pseudobulk from 104 donors (275 runs) in nine lineages. A gene was detected in a donor-lineage with at least 5 counts and at least 10 CPM, and in a lineage when detected in at least half of the eligible donors. A donor was eligible with at least 20 cells and 10,000 counts in that lineage (25 to 95 eligible donors per lineage).

Controls were known liver and plasma proteins and lineage markers (liver 7 of 7, plasma 8 of 8, single cell 10 of 11 listed markers); olfactory-receptor genes as negatives (1 of 387 detected in the liver proteome, 0 of 387 in plasma and on the CosMx panel, 0 of 292 measurable in single cell); and a rise in detection with bulk RNA abundance (highest minus lowest decile at least 0.30; liver 0.815, single cell 0.727, plasma 0.123). A field failing the known-answer, negative or coverage check would not be released. A field failing the abundance check is released with a note, which applied to plasma. There is no confirmatory test. Absence of detection is recorded as untestable, not negative.

*Liver protein coverage by class.* A gene was counted as quantified when the PXD051911 liver differential table (6,071 proteins; 58 participants, 12 control and 46 MASLD) contained a row for it after symbol updating with HGNChelper (90 symbols remapped) [ref: HGNChelper]. Coverage is a count by Catalog class, and no test was performed. Controls: 14 of 16 known liver proteins were quantified (PNPLA3 and TM6SF2 absent), 3 of 10 tissue-restricted non-liver proteins were quantified, and 89 of 89 remapped symbols were confirmed by an independent alias table. Detection in any sample (previous field) is a broader criterion than a row in the differential table.

*SteatoSITE stage replication.* For each Catalog gene that is a discovery F3–F4 versus F0–F1 call, the SteatoSITE F3–F4 log2FC (see Held-out replication in SteatoSITE) was classed as replicated (discovery sign, BH q < 0.05 within kept genes), opposite sign (opposite sign, q < 0.05), not replicated at adequate power (q ≥ 0.05 and per-gene minimum detectable effect at most the expected effect, the absolute discovery log2FC times the mean leave-one-cohort-out shrinkage factor, 0.845), underpowered (otherwise) or not measurable (removed by the SteatoSITE expression filter). Other genes are recorded as not a discovery call or not in the release universe.

*Follow-up annotation.* For every Catalog gene, we added gnomAD v4.1 LOEUF and pLI for the Ensembl MANE Select transcript (deciles over 17,325 genes) [ref: gnomAD v4.1], the DepMap Public 24Q4 common-essential call and median Chronos gene effect [ref: DepMap], and the Human Protein Atlas secretome location and blood concentrations [ref: Human Protein Atlas]. These fields describe how a gene might behave in a follow-up experiment and do not enter any evidence class or route. Controls used core-essential genes (CEGv2) and nonessential genes (NEGv1). The lowest LOEUF decile held 0.285 of core-essential genes against 0.024 of nonessential genes, and 0.914 of core-essential against 0.006 of nonessential genes were DepMap common-essential. All 10 known blood-secreted proteins tested were annotated as secreted to blood. No test was performed.

### Predictive-model evaluation

*Single-cell encoders.* The single-cell encoder comparison maps five broad cell classes, not disease stages. TranscriptFormer TF-Sapiens exposure is tied to the exact evaluated checkpoint (SHA-256 eff027e7393f92a32cadcbb2e13a917e24b62b71341571bf40f38ff6ec6d3021) through all 30 prediction-shard records and the 50,000-cell embedding record [ref: TranscriptFormer]. Its complete inventory of 644 human training datasets shows five Resource studies absent from training and two seen in training (GSE185477 and GSE192742). Pooled scores and displayed gains keep all seven studies and cannot be read as evidence from unseen studies only. Geneformer V2 has no qualified unseen study among those seven [ref: Geneformer]. The TranscriptFormer comparison restricted to unseen studies uses the original equal-study mean over five studies and 80 biological donor identifiers. Its 10,000 saved paired draws resample donors within study (seed 20260824) and preserve evaluable class coverage; cells are not resampled as independent units. Intervals are conditional on the fixed models, five studies, class coverage and observed cells, and do not estimate generalization to a population of studies. No models or scores were refitted for this comparison.

*Severity model populations.* Model-specific training and evaluation populations are reported separately from the population of the Resource differential-expression analysis. The released severity model card records 521 training sample records and no complete donor key. We do not describe these as 521 verified participants or as the bulk differential-expression roster. The released scorer applies its stored expression transformation and weights without refitting to evaluation outcomes. Its GSE268273 evaluation uses 109 participant records. Figure 7D evaluates GSE213621 in 263 genetic identities (one record each, after removal of 11 identities whose two libraries carry different stage labels) and GSE276114 as 81 MASLD and 82 chronic viral hepatitis participants. The published record-level contrasts use 299 disease-only and 367 control-inclusive GSE213621 records and 177 GSE276114 records. These are two additional cohorts and three contrasts, not three independent cohorts. Source-declared participant keys and joins of counts to phenotypes were checked; genetic identity was not verified.

*Exposure of evaluation cohorts.* Whether a cohort is external depends on the model. GSE213621 also contributes to Resource analyses, and all 109 GSE268273 records occur in BulkFormer pretraining [ref: BulkFormer]. The latter limits an unseen-sample claim for BulkFormer, not for the separately fitted severity scorer. Cohorts used to inspect refusal thresholds or other acceptance criteria are exposed to development for those criteria, and GSE268273 is exposed to development for the choice of expression transform. Four arms were eligible: each has a coherent expression transform and can score one sample at a time (out-of-cohort Spearman 0.6506 to 0.6631 on these 109 records). Among them, the released scorer is the one with the highest Spearman, a rule from the original prespecification. We amended the protocol to make coherence and reliability preconditions for eligibility after results for the first three transform families had been seen and before the quantile-normalization family, which the released scorer uses, had been run. Input coverage, score refusals and diagnostics are reported separately, and a diagnostic threshold not shipped with the scorer is not described as an enforced refusal rule.

*Comparison with in-cohort models.* We compared severity transfer with the recorded in-cohort ridge on the same evaluation population. In the three additional contrasts, the native comparator used full-cohort expression scaling before outer-fold evaluation, although feature selection and PCA were fitted on outer-training records. Those preprocessing steps were not refitted within each inner tuning split, so this comparator is transductive rather than a strictly inductive nested-validation estimate. The 10,000-draw stage-stratified paired participant bootstrap used seed 213621. Its intervals are conditional on the fitted predictions and do not include uncertainty from retraining. Participant-level predictions of the native comparator were not saved, so these paired intervals are source-reported rather than independently recomputed. An interval that includes zero does not establish equivalence.

*Agreement metrics.* Spearman correlation measures ordering, and its ratio to a tied-label ceiling is labeled separately from the raw correlation. Neither establishes agreement with histological stage or calibration on an absolute stage scale. The repeated-biopsy ICC comparison uses 58 source-declared GSE193066 pairs whose identities are not verified. It is a source-reported diagnostic, not primary evidence of within-person reliability or a calibration of label error. Possible biological change and shared technical effects further limit it, and a larger ICC alone does not establish greater accuracy. The model card reports that only three of these pairs have both biopsies within the released scorer's acceptance bounds. The 58-pair ICC therefore describes scoring outside the scorer's stated input limits, not performance on accepted inputs, and it was not recalculated here. Its stage SD of 0.6433 and the separately reported PXD051911 value of 0.4655 are inputs to sensitivity scenarios. They are not an empirically qualified 0.4655–0.6433 label-error range or a calibrated severity ceiling.

*RNA to H3K27ac.* For RNA–H3K27ac prediction, we checked the 99 participant keys and five fixed folds. The source rank-4 model uses training-fold preprocessing and a baseline equal to the training-target mean. Its recorded squared-error skill and uncertainty were not recomputed from participant losses. The forward and reverse skills are measured in different target spaces, so they cannot be compared to rank the models.

*Reporter-assay rows.* The Figure 7B reporter-assay rows score 1,033 GSE281364 elements in the HepG2 control and palmitate-oleate contexts only (tanh of the mean Fisher-z Spearman over the two contexts; 10,000 resamples of 239 long-range blocks) [ref: GSE281364 source study]. LX-2 control and TGF-beta outcomes are in the same outcome table but were not scored. The comparators are a prespecified ladder of ridge baselines: allele identity (B0, 0.134), CpG (B1, 0.178), sequence composition (B2, 0.176) and flank and dinucleotide features (B3, 0.167); HyenaDNA scores 0.216. The HyenaDNA gain over B3 is 0.049 (95% interval 0.0006 to 0.099; Bonferroni-7 lower bound -0.015), and over B1 0.038 (95% interval -0.006 to 0.083), so the corrected gain holds only against allele identity. gkm-SVM was also scored (0.126). MPRALegNet was scored on a different endpoint (0.204 and 0.200), so its values are not comparable. The Bonferroni family is the seven displayed reporter-assay rows, and the corrected lower bound is the 0.025/7 quantile of the same paired bootstrap draws. HyenaDNA's corrected lower bound stays above zero (+0.006) when the family is widened to all eleven fitted pretrained heads [ref: HyenaDNA]. A HyenaDNA head trained on the Currin caQTL leads (gain 0.130) was not adopted and is not reported in the figures.

*Scope.* Of the transfer metrics, four were reproduced from saved outputs; the others are source-reported comparisons or unresolved checks [model-evaluation summary table; number to be assigned]. No new model was trained for this assessment. Linear regression can use log-expression predictors; the transformation changes the relationship modeled and does not invalidate regression. Results for one scVI task do not imply that linear models are generally superior. A separate exploratory development (a rank-12 RNA-to-H3K27ac model with local correction, its acquisition and input-robustness checks, and an AlphaGenome allele analysis against Currin caQTL effects) is not part of Figure 7, and no result from it is reported in this paper.

## Quantification and statistical analysis

### Statistics and reproducibility

Biological donors or participants are the unit of inference in every test, except where a test is explicitly at the level of genes, loci or credible sets. Those units are comparison units, not replicates (see Units of inference). Each test reports its analysis universe, numerator, denominator, effect size and uncertainty where available. BH adjustment was applied within prespecified analysis families, and permutation p values used the plus-one correction. Null results and failed acceptance criteria were kept and used to limit the wording of claims. Nominal p values are never described as significant.

Primary definitions are distinguished from sensitivity analyses. Primary transcriptomic evidence is the pooled limma-voom quality-weighted result at BH adjusted p < 0.05 and |log2FC| > 0.50, with the effect-size-aware interval-null FDR gate kept as a comparator. The genetic analysis has two coverage layers across the 35 prespecified tier-1/2 GWAS strata: ABF colocalization for every testable gene–GWAS pair, and multi-signal SuSiE colocalization only where its prerequisites are met. The evidence classes use the SuSiE call, and the ABF map and the SuSiE-or-ABF union are reported with their own denominators.
