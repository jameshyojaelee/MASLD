To compare inherited genetic evidence with disease-state evidence gene by gene, we assembled a multimodal human MASLD Resource. It records trait-associated inherited variation, multicohort disease remodeling, and donor-aware cell and tissue context side by side (Figure 1A). For each result, the Resource lists the source cohort, assay, phenotype, biological unit and set of genes tested, whether the same data entered another analysis or source study, the evidence state, the main remaining alternative explanation, and the next discriminating experiment (Figure 1A). The transcriptomic data comprise 1,257 libraries that passed technical quality control, from nine cohorts (GSE126848, GSE130970, GSE135251, GSE162694, GSE167523, GSE174478, GSE193066, GSE213621 and GSE240729). The pooled disease-versus-control model uses 774 genetic-identity clusters from the five cohorts that include controls (138 control and 636 disease clusters). These clusters are not verified independent donors. The genetic data span prespecified liver GWAS strata from several ancestry groups. We kept direct MASLD and liver-imaging traits separate from the liver enzymes ALT, AST, and GGT, which we treat as proxy phenotypes. Donor-collapsed single-cell data, prespecified gene programs, and liver protein, chromatin-accessibility, and spatial measurements add context in each assay's native units. They are not extra votes in a single target score. Throughout, we define replication by participants or biological donors; cells, spots, sequencing runs, guides, and arrays do not add to the biological sample size.

We first asked how often the two maps name the same gene. After applying the shared-posterior eligibility check to carried-forward negative results, 3,589 stable-Ensembl genes were evaluated by multi-signal colocalization under the existing priority rule (Figure 1B). Of these, 426 were colocalized with a liver eQTL (PP.H4 > 0.5, where PP.H4 is the posterior probability that the GWAS and eQTL associations share one variant), 209 were canonical disease-state genes (BH q < 0.05 and |log2FC| > 0.5 in the pooled disease-versus-control fit), and 26 were in both. Within expression deciles, 21.4 shared genes were expected (Mantel-Haenszel OR 1.282, 95% CI 0.826 to 1.988; exact stratified *p* = 0.277). The test definition is unchanged; the eligibility qualification was adopted on November 6. The interval includes 1. Historical expression-matching diagnostics in the earlier 3,769-gene universe did not fully control expression, and they were not rerun on the qualified universe. We therefore state no direction from this overlap (Supplementary Note 3).

Several groups of genes fall outside this comparison. In the 14,704-gene native RNA–genetics interface, 10,928 genes have no multi-signal COLOC result and 187 have a priority SuSiE-untestable state; these groups contain 935 and 11 canonical disease-state genes, respectively. Neither group is counted as a genetic negative. The priority rule makes a gene without a positive call untestable when any tier-1/2 study fails the shared-posterior eligibility check, even if another study has a finite PP.H4 below threshold. A sensitivity in which any eligible study makes the gene testable instead contains 3,736 evaluated genes, with 426 colocalized, 217 disease-state calls and 26 shared genes (21.1 expected; Mantel-Haenszel OR 1.30, 95% CI 0.84 to 2.02; exact *p* = 0.27). This alternative aggregation does not define the adopted classes. Grouping by gene symbol gives 14,720 rows; the annotation-adjudicated Gene Catalog has 14,502 jointly eligible identities after the priority qualification. These denominators are not interchangeable. Supplementary Note 5 reports a separate signed comparison over 14,703 genes between genetically predicted liver expression for ALT and the disease-versus-control statistic [ref: OTTERS TWAS method].

We next asked which liver traits supply the colocalization support (Figure 1C). We counted support from any eligible SuSiE gene-trait pair, not only from the strongest trait. Of the 436 primary SuSiE genes (426 of which fall in the 3,589-gene comparison), 414 were supported only by liver-enzyme GWAS, 12 only by direct-disease or liver-fat GWAS, and 10 by both. The two trait classes were not tested equally. Of the 414 enzyme-only genes, 46 had a finite recorded SuSiE output in a NAFLD, NASH, or MRI proton density fat fraction (PDFF) GWAS and 368 did not. Of the 12 direct-only genes, 11 had a finite recorded enzyme-GWAS output. Among 362 genes with finite recorded output in both trait classes, 56 were enzyme-supported and 21 direct-supported, including 10 supported by both. These availability counts describe stored r2 output before qualification of carried negatives; they do not define current gene-level applicability or establish pair-specific eligibility. We describe the fine-mapped architecture of each trait class in Figure 2.

The Resource is organized as a graph of source dependencies and an analysis workflow that ends in a decision for each gene (Figure 1D). Figure 2 maps phenotype- and ancestry-aware inherited variation and its candidate molecular links. Figure 3 defines multicohort, cross-sectional disease remodeling and where it meets genetics. Figure 4 reports donor-level program scores in five cell lineages with their bulk-tissue context; transcription-factor activity is in the supplement. Figure 5 places candidate genes and programs in molecular and physical tissue context. Figure 6 organizes the two maps in the MASLD Gene Catalog and names the smallest experiment that could resolve each remaining alternative. Figure 7 asks what predictive models learn from the Resource. No step requires agreement across all modalities, and no aggregate rank is used as a calibrated measure of biological truth.

Two sets of examples recur through these figures. GNMT, MAT1A, and CYP2C19 connect the RNA, cross-sectional stage, and protein views of hepatocyte metabolic remodeling. The ECM/IGFBP7 and ductular-injury/BICC1 programs connect prespecified donor-level programs to spatial interpretation against matched nulls. We report the colocalization state of each example, but that state does not determine its role in disease remodeling.

Taken together, this design lets a reader trace why a gene was nominated, see what has been tested for it, and choose an experiment that could distinguish the remaining alternatives. None of these steps depends on the outcome of a perturbation screen. A separate prospective perturbation study may use the Resource, but its gene list, guides, phenotypes, hits, and mechanisms are not part of this paper.
