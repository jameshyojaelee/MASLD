## A 102-donor single-cell atlas contains 117 prespecified gene programs

To place disease-associated gene programs in specific liver cell types, we integrated seven single-cell datasets (275 libraries, 1,232,318 cells) into one annotated atlas [ref: source studies of the seven single-cell datasets] (Figure 4A). After two prespecified exclusions of low-cell libraries, the analyzed atlas has 273 libraries (1,232,285 cells) from 102 donors. It carries 117 prespecified gene programs discovered in lineage subsets, weighted sets of co-expressed genes [ref: Hotspot], across five tested lineages. We summarized program, communication and regulator analyses at the donor level, because sequencing runs, sort fractions and cells are not biological replicates. Source-qualified descriptive categories are 17 Cirrhosis, 23 Healthy, 36 Steatohepatitis, 14 Steatosis, 12 Unresolved. Deposited diagnosis, measured histology and etiology were kept separate. GSE202379 P98 was excluded from disease inference because its Healthy-control label conflicts with SAF S1A1F1. P30 (S0A1F0) was retained as a qualified nonsteatotic reference, with an exclusion sensitivity; it is not confirmed histologically normal tissue. Official RNA/GSM metadata place local GSE244832 D11/JB_321 in MASL and D14/JB_337 in MASH. This correction does not verify RNA–ATAC pair identities. All 19 GSE192742 donors remain outside disease inference. Alcohol/PBC cirrhosis is not classified as MASLD, and GSE189600 replicate independence remains unresolved.

The atlas follows the same evidence rules as the rest of the Resource. A program received an evidence state only when donor identity, retained gene weight, cell-state coverage and complete multiple-testing families were all in place. If any one failed, the program was untestable. Untestable is not evidence that the biological program is absent. Each selected program keeps its unresolved alternative and its next discriminating experiment. The integrated UMAP shows 50,000 cells sampled uniformly from the analyzed cells and colored by cell type (Figure 4B). It is descriptive, and cells are not the unit of inference. We do not draw a donor-averaged disease density, because its Healthy and Steatohepatitis groups come from different mixes of datasets.

## Gene content contradicts the discovery lineage for 37 of 117 programs

A program's name records the cell-type subset in which it was discovered, not what its genes are. To test whether the genes match the name, we scored each program against donor-level expression in each lineage. Content matched the declared lineage for 80 of 117 programs and contradicted it for 37 (Figure 4D). Across 1,000 donor bootstrap draws, 76 matches and 34 mismatches kept the same call in at least 95% of draws, and 7 programs were unstable (10 when donors were resampled within each lineage). The per-draw match count had a central 95% range of 77 to 80. The mismatches are systematic. Of the 37, 17 were discovered outside hepatocytes but have hepatocyte content, and several are named for it. Examples are a "Hepatocyte xenobiotic (CYP2C)" program labelled fibroblast and a "Bile-acid transport (ABCB11)" program labelled cholangiocyte. These 17 programs carry a higher measured burden of ambient RNA, the free transcripts captured along with each cell, than correctly labelled non-hepatocyte programs (median 0.432 against 0.061). Hepatocyte transcripts are the dominant ambient species in these data. This association does not show that ambient RNA explains every mismatch. The panels separate three lineages for each program: the lineage its content matches, the lineage it was discovered in and the lineage used for disease testing. The program definitions are unchanged.

In separate donor-paired contrasts within the same atlas, Stromal ECM/IGFBP7 scored highest in fibroblasts (beta = 2.271 over hepatocytes; 53 donors) and Ductular injury/BICC1 scored highest in cholangiocytes (beta = 1.574 over hepatocytes; 51 donors) [figure panel to be assigned]. Both pass the ten-contrast BH and HC3 families [ref: HC3 heteroskedasticity-consistent standard errors]. This is descriptive localization. It does not show where the transcripts came from or that the program acts within that cell type.

## No program shows a supported ordinal stage association in two multi-stage single-cell datasets

Two focal programs, Stromal ECM (IGFBP7) and Ductular injury (BICC1), were discovered in hepatocyte profiles but are among the 37 whose content contradicts that label. We tested all 117 fixed programs for cross-sectional association with Healthy/Steatosis/Steatohepatitis categories after correcting source diagnoses. The ambient-correctable universe contains at most 59 source-qualified donors across four datasets; the two multi-stage datasets, GSE202379 and GSE244832, supply the fitted slopes (37 cholangiocyte, 42 fibroblast, 54 hepatocyte, 24 macrophage, 52 T-cell donors). We fitted score ~ ordinal stage + dataset by generalized least squares (REML; nlme::gls with varIdent; Pinheiro et al.), with a separate residual variance per dataset and 19,999 within-dataset stage permutations (seed 20260923). No program passes BH correction over 117 programs plus the unchanged stability rule (smallest q = 0.181). Ductular injury has beta = 0.207 (nominal 95% CI 0.084 to 0.329; permutation p = 0.00155, BH q = 0.181); Stromal ECM has beta = 0.162 (nominal 95% CI 0.011 to 0.313; permutation p = 0.03945, BH q = 0.243) (Figure 4C). The two cohort slopes have opposite signs for 58 programs; 0 heterogeneity tests pass BH over 117 (smallest q = 0.173). The variance model was chosen after earlier focal-program slopes and equal-variance results were known. These corrected estimates remain conditional on two cohorts, their source diagnoses and the ordinal coding. Failure to pass correction does not establish absent effects.

An exploratory November 2 discovery check did not recover either focal program when GSE244832 was omitted: the best top-50 overlaps were two Stromal ECM genes and zero Ductular injury genes. Their member genes nevertheless co-vary across 45 GSE202379 donors (Holm p=0.002 each), as do 27 of the other 28 hepatocyte programs. Their discovery therefore depends on GSE244832, while donor-level covariance outside that dataset does not establish a hepatocyte origin; ambient RNA or doublet burden can also produce it.

We next examined ambient RNA using unchanged scores, original full-transcriptome exposures and fixed program weights in the correction-available donor universe. Stromal ECM changes from 0.4207 to 0.1525 in the paired equal-variance analysis (delta -0.2682, nominal p = 0.00148, BH q = 0.173); its ambient-corrected GLS beta is 0.077 (permutation BH q = 0.331). Ductular injury changes from 0.4574 to 0.4504 in the paired equal-variance analysis (delta -0.0070, nominal p = 0.8588, BH q = 1.000); its ambient-corrected GLS beta is 0.172 (permutation BH q = 0.111). The GLS sensitivity tests 106 non-T-cell programs within the 117-program family and selects 0 (smallest q = 0.111). The 11 T-cell programs are untestable on the corrected native scale. Attenuation classification is conditional on primary selection, so it applies to neither focal program. These results do not establish the cellular origin of either program's transcripts.

We then tested the two programs in independent donors from GSE256398 [ref: GSE256398 source study], using single-nucleus hepatocyte pseudobulk. The models were score ~ stage + sex + age and score ~ disease + sex + age, with HC3 standard errors and Holm correction over four tests [figure panel to be assigned]. No test passed Holm correction. For Ductular injury, the stage beta was 0.694 (95% CI 0.062 to 1.326; 13 non-cirrhotic donors; Holm *p* = 0.139). Its disease-versus-healthy beta was 1.080 (−0.091 to 2.250; Holm *p* = 0.203) in 17 donors, and the 11 disease donors in that contrast include 4 with MASH cirrhosis. For Stromal ECM, the two betas were 0.300 (−0.709 to 1.309) and 0.176 (−0.840 to 1.192), Holm *p* = 1. In the prespecified random-set control, 87% of 1,000 expression-matched hepatocyte gene sets rose with stage, and 8.4% and 4.1% of them exceeded the Ductular injury stage and disease t statistics. With 13 and 17 donors these tests have low power, and no positive control was run.

We compared steatohepatitis with steatosis within each focal program's content-associated lineage, using the same correction-available cells and donors and the corrected RNA diagnoses. Ductular injury in cholangiocytes: beta 0.135 (HC3-based meta-analysis 95% CI -0.075 to 0.346; n = 26 donors; two-program BH q = 0.208). Stromal ECM in fibroblasts: beta 0.321 (HC3-based meta-analysis 95% CI 0.086 to 0.556; n = 26 donors; two-program BH q = 0.015). These are targeted projected-expression tests in GSE202379 and GSE244832, with a 50-cell primary gate; they do not independently replicate the hepatocyte discovery or exclude within-lineage substate mixtures. Cohort-specific estimates, full-raw, 20-cell and annotation-confidence sensitivities are reported separately. Captured-lineage fractions are not whole-tissue proportions.

A supplementary donor-level communication screen contains 55,402 native ligand-receptor/cell-type groups: 374 are fitted and 55,028 have insufficient stage counts. It retains the native within-cell-type-pair/term BH families followed by across-cell-pair Bonferroni correction. The separate fixed 26-test program screen has 20 estimable tests. One Ductular injury association passes model-based and HC3 BH: hepatocyte CDH1–endothelial PTPRM, beta = 0.4984, n = 57, q = 9.94e-06, HC3 q = 6e-10. Its sign agrees in 3.0 of 4 cohort omissions (coefficient range -0.0170 to 0.6873), so cohort stability is limited. These same-atlas RNA associations do not establish ligand secretion, receptor activation or signaling. Of four selected transcription-factor regulons, two remain untestable because of insufficient retained weight.

## Three bulk cohorts do not resolve linear fibrosis or NAS associations of program scores

To test whether program scores track histology in bulk tissue, we scored all 112 eligible weighted programs in the 364 genetic-identity clusters from the three cohorts of the 774-unit dataset that record both fibrosis and NAS (GSE130970, GSE135251 and GSE162694) (Figure 4E). Within each cohort, we regressed each score on sex, fibrosis stage and NAS. We combined the cohort estimates by random-effects meta-analysis with the Knapp-Hartung adjustment [ref: Knapp-Hartung] and applied BH correction over 224 tests on the two axes. No program met BH q < 0.05 on either axis (smallest q 0.079 for fibrosis and 0.159 for NAS), with or without composition adjustment under either set of cell-type fractions. With three cohorts, the Knapp-Hartung test uses t on 2 degrees of freedom, so it has low power. Without composition adjustment, the nearest BH rejection missed by a factor of 1.6 in *p*: four rejections needed a fourth-smallest *p* of 0.00089 or less, and it was 0.0014. In the same analysis, Knapp-Hartung 95% intervals exclude zero for 34 fibrosis and 12 NAS programs. These intervals are not adjusted for the 224 tests, and with three cohorts their width depends mainly on each program's between-cohort Q. The smallest *p* values therefore go to programs whose cohort estimates agree closely, not necessarily to the largest effects: all 12 of the largest |t| values have Q of 0.49 or less, against an expected value of 2. NAS associations are shown in Figure S4 [panel letter to be assigned]. Adding 93 GSE174478 source records, which lie outside the 774-unit dataset and are not identity-resolved, raises the meta-analysis from three to four cohorts and gives 16 fibrosis calls and one NAS call. After composition adjustment, three fibrosis calls remain with InstaPrism cell-type fractions estimated from the fragment counts used here (fragment-count fractions) and one with fractions estimated from an earlier count matrix of the same cohorts (earlier-matrix fractions). All of these calls depend on the untruncated Knapp-Hartung variance. Each has a between-cohort Q below its expected value, and none remains when that variance is floored at the REML value. Figure 4E therefore does not resolve whether bulk program scores track linear fibrosis category or NAS. Grouped by gene-content lineage, the fibrosis estimates are positive for 20 of 21 cholangiocyte-content and 19 of 21 fibroblast-content programs and negative for 29 of 36 hepatocyte-content programs (Kruskal-Wallis *p* = 3.7 × 10⁻¹¹ by content lineage against 0.010 by discovery lineage; descriptive). Bulk program shifts with fibrosis therefore mostly reflect a change in cell mix: more bile-duct and fibroblast RNA and less hepatocyte RNA. Calls appear with z-based inference (Figure 4E legend) or with GSE174478 added and the untruncated Knapp-Hartung variance, and none holds across these analysis choices. A linear coding of category does not capture nonlinear remodeling or change within a person. A matched-background comparison of unweighted scores does not establish disease specificity (Supplementary Note 9).

## Program scores covary across bulk livers in two cohorts not used to build the program map

We next asked whether program scores covary with one another across bulk livers [figure panel to be assigned]. In the four discovery cohorts, 2,426 of 6,195 tested pairs of the 113 bulk-scorable programs had a positive partial correlation that passed BH correction, after adjustment for sex, fibrosis stage and NAS and after removal of the leading residual component. Of these, 650 pairs join programs of the same lineage and 1,776 join programs of different lineages. This discovery step used 469 source records from GSE130970, GSE135251, GSE162694 and GSE174478, scored on counts that precede the identity-qualified 774-unit dataset. Its unit is therefore not the genetic-identity cluster used in the other bulk analyses. In bulk tissue, covariance of program scores mixes cell-type abundance, biopsy content and program activity, so it does not show that cell types coordinate. A prespecified test in 258 GSE213621 disease units (genetic-identity clusters) used 2,328 pairs rediscovered on 371 identity-qualified discovery units, and it was untestable. Its program-group permutation control keeps covariance within each program and removes it between programs. This control still gave a mean excess over the non-coordinated baseline of 0.025 (95th percentile 0.081; prespecified limits 0.02 and 0.05), so the run stopped before any GSE213621 outcome statistic was computed. An earlier GSE193066 comparison rests on participant identities that cannot be qualified and is not counted as replication (Supplementary Note 10).

We then tested the 2,426 fixed discovery pairs, with a separate prespecified test and null, in two bulk cohorts that were not used to build or test the program map: GSE281797 (91 participants) and GSE225740 (87 participants), one biopsy each [ref: GSE281797 and GSE225740 source studies]. In both cohorts, the fixed pairs had positive histology-adjusted partial correlations more often than pairs placed at random among programs of the same lineages. This held after removing 1 and after removing 3 residual components. In each cohort the largest of the permutation and bootstrap *p* values was 0.000999, the floor set by 1,000 permutations and 1,000 bootstrap draws, and the null was rejected in both cohorts under Holm correction over the two. With 1 component removed, the fraction of pairs with a positive correlation exceeded the random-placement mean by 0.158 in GSE281797 (0.848 against 0.690; participant-bootstrap 95% interval of the excess 0.132 to 0.182) and by 0.312 in GSE225740 (0.834 against 0.522; 0.270 to 0.323) (Supplementary Note 10). The program-group permutation control that stopped the GSE213621 test was not run in these two cohorts. The two excesses are about 6 and 12 times the mean excess that control gave in GSE213621 (0.025), but that control used a different statistic and baseline, so the comparison is approximate. Participant overlap with the discovery cohorts was not checked by genotype in either cohort. GSE225740 identities cannot be checked at all, because it has no reads and no deposited sex, age or batch. GSE281797 is therefore the stronger replication, because it has full covariates and verifiable sex. In GSE225740, the result held after we added three estimated composition components, log library size and the top-100-gene count share to the design. The GSE281797 composition estimate failed its prespecified quality rule and was not read, so the GSE281797 result has no composition adjustment. None of these numbers is pooled with any GSE213621 or GSE193066 value. Estimated composition is not measured composition, so these results do not show that the covariance is independent of cell-type abundance.

To test whether the same pairs covary within tissue, we used Visium spots in 32 HMSMA livers [ref: HMSMA spatial transcriptomics source study] and the 44 programs measurable in every array. Each donor contributed one array. Three arrays inferred to come from controls were excluded. The source study lists 7 controls among its 35 individuals without a key to the arrays, so 4 of the 32 analyzed donors are probably controls that cannot be identified. Program pairs with positive bulk covariance co-vary across spots, but coordinated pairs do not exceed other positively covarying pairs (134 against 80 pairs; excess 0.0116 Fisher z, *p* = 0.124; an excess of 0.0280 or more would have been detected with 80% power). Among cross-lineage pairs that share no genes, the 160 coordinated pairs correlated more across spots than the 372 non-coordinated pairs of the same lineage pairs (excess 0.0257 Fisher z; Holm *p* = 0.0002; positive in 31 of 32 livers) (Supplementary Note 10). However, 292 of those 372 non-coordinated pairs have a gene-disjoint bulk correlation of zero or below. This difference therefore tracks the sign of bulk covariance, not coordination beyond the leading residual component. Each 55-micron spot holds several cells, so this is co-location at spot scale. It does not show expression in different cells or contact between cells. HMSMA has no key linking arrays to clinical records, so it carries no stage information. Neither the bulk nor the spot results show coordination between cell types.

## Stromal ECM and Ductular injury scores increase along a continuous molecular axis after stage adjustment

We then asked whether the two focal programs vary along a continuous molecular axis when recorded fibrosis stage is kept in the model. In the 367 disease units of GSE162694 and GSE213621, both Stromal ECM and Ductular injury increase along the fixed external projection in stage- and sex-adjusted models at the genetic-cluster level (meta coefficients 0.760 and 0.707 score SD per continuum SD). Cell-type composition explains 80% and 82% of within-stage continuum variance. The cohort-PC1 score gives concordant effects. These two continuum scores come from closely related methods (within-cohort correlations approximately 0.999), so they are not independent corroboration. After cell-type composition adjustment, the Ductular injury meta coefficient changes little (0.60 to 0.73 across adjustments and fraction sets). Its GSE162694 estimate, however, falls from 0.952 to 0.477 with the fragment-count fractions and to 0.639 with the earlier-matrix fractions. The Stromal ECM meta coefficient falls to 0.203 to 0.479, depending on the deconvolution input. In GSE162694 alone, Stromal ECM is not supported with the fragment-count fractions (0.172, 95% CI −0.032 to 0.377; BH q 0.233) (Figure S4 [panel letter to be assigned]).

An exploratory November 2 control on the same 367 disease units reproduced the focal coefficients but found comparable values for expression-matched random axes. On the fixed projection, random-axis medians were 0.612 for Stromal ECM and 0.538 for Ductular injury, with 95th percentiles 0.804 and 0.736; Holm p values over the four program-by-axis tests were 0.43–0.46. Random axes called a median of 93 of 117 programs against 94 observed. These associations describe a shared disease-expression gradient, not specificity for the continuum signature. The random-axis comparison did not test the 43 systems.

Figure 4F shows descriptive fixed-window score profiles for both programs in all five Resource cohorts and has no inferential panel. The stage- and sex-adjusted estimates are reported separately (Figure S4 [panel letter to be assigned]). GSE126848, GSE130970 and GSE135251 overlap the construction of the continuum and are shown apart from GSE162694 and GSE213621, the two cohorts independent of that construction. GSE126848 is omitted from stage-stratified panels, because recorded fibrosis is unavailable. GSE213621 records Control, F0–F1, F2 and F3–F4, which are not exact F0–F4 stages. Exact-stage estimates and a paired sensitivity to coarsened histology are reported separately for GSE162694. The complete 117-program family, transcript comparisons, pathway collections and ten fixed NMF axes are shown in Figures S3M–S3U and S4H–S4N. Their alignment with the fibrosis-based results supports reading the two together. Histology anchors severity, and the continuum describes a shared axis of molecular variation among samples within the same recorded histologic category. This alignment does not establish a temporal order or replace the fixed program definitions.

## Forty-three gene-membership systems share one diffuse continuum association

To describe remodeling within a recorded stage by groups of features rather than gene by gene, we grouped 13,092 correlated features into a few dozen auditable systems (Figure 4G). Before any stage or continuum outcome was read, gene membership with the continuum genes excluded placed 5,439 nodes in one graph component and 43 size-ranked systems. Six nodes in three disconnected two-node components remain listed but are outside the system summaries. The systems extend the focal view to the complete testable pathway and Hotspot layers. They are not independent. The 43 system scores behave like about 14 independent systems, and their first principal component carries 41% of the variance (all 466 GSE162694 and GSE213621 units, including their 99 controls; descriptive). Memberships, weights and coordinates were built on an earlier 23,370-gene expression universe and are unchanged. System scores use the 24,115 genes tested in the 774-unit data. Because 392 genes of the earlier universe are absent from these data, four nodes keep less than 80% of their members and are untestable. Figure 4G shows the 5,435 testable stage-adjusted continuum coefficients as system strips, with system medians and nominal donor-bootstrap intervals; the membership map is in Figure S4Q. Coefficients use the 367 stage-complete disease units (109 GSE162694 and 258 GSE213621). The 68 GSE213621 controls have no recorded histology and are excluded. In the donor-first estimate, we fitted each participant's collection-balanced system score within each cohort and combined the two cohorts by fixed-effect meta-analysis. The feature-first summary instead takes the median node coefficient within each system and collection and averages over collections. Across the 43 systems, the donor-first stage and continuum estimates have Spearman rho = 0.67 (bootstrap 95% interval 0.38 to 0.84), and 34 share direction. The feature-first summaries give 0.63 and 32. Broad molecular summaries therefore align across stage and continuum, while cohort-specific window shapes remain visible (Figures S4Q–S4U). These are descriptive summaries over nested gene sets and overlapping windows. They are not system-level significance tests, a temporal ordering or a universal ontology.

Pathway similarity networks partitioned into modules are an established way to summarize MASLD transcriptomes (Kumak et al., 2026), and we claim no novelty for that construction. What differs here is the order of operations. We built the 43 systems from gene membership that excludes the continuum genes before reading any stage or continuum outcome, and the 117 Hotspot programs keep memberships and weights fixed in advance. The geometry therefore could not have been selected by the outcome it is used to describe. We did not use centrality in a curated protein interaction database to rank genes or programs at any point, because degree in such a database reflects annotation depth as well as biology. An equal-cohort pooled view of all 43 systems is shown beside the five cohort-specific window maps (Figure S4U; Supplementary Note 11).

Figure 4H compares two coefficients for each system: the fibrosis-stage coefficient (score SD per cohort SD of the recorded stage code) and the stage- and sex-adjusted continuum coefficient (score SD per continuum SD). Both are estimated donor-first in the 367 disease units, with 95% donor-bootstrap intervals on both axes. For 22 systems, both intervals exclude zero. The two coefficients estimate different quantities. Two further kinds of test were run on 435 units that included the 68 GSE213621 controls as a separate stage category. The maxT test uses permutations within stage-by-sex strata and compares each system's statistic with the largest statistic across all 43 systems in each permutation, which controls the family-wise error rate. The competitive tests ask whether a system is more strongly associated than random feature sets of the same size, drawn either as connected sets from the prespecified 30-nearest-neighbor feature graph (the connected-region null) or uniformly from all features (the more permissive uniform size-matched null). On that roster, no system exceeds the connected-region null on both co-primary continuum scores (the fixed external projection and cohort-PC1). One system exceeds the uniform null: S01, the largest system (311 features), whose representative terms are integrin binding, extracellular matrix organization, contractile myofibroblast and cell adhesion. Eighteen systems pass maxT in both cohorts on both co-primary scores. The two scores give system coefficients correlated at r = 0.999, so this is in effect a two-cohort criterion. In the 367 disease units, the unadjusted fit calls all 18 (BH q < 0.05 within 43 systems), but 14 of the 18 lose the call in at least one of 11 estimable composition-adjusted fits, and 9 are called in 5 or fewer. A maxT test that combines both cohorts in one permutation family gives 33 of 43 on the 435 units. Because the system scores are strongly correlated and none exceeds the connected-region null, we report these counts as parts of one diffuse shared axis, not as 33 or 18 separate findings.

Retrospective transfer checks in GSE174478 and GSE240729 did not show continuum-fibrosis correlations beyond those of matched random gene sets (Supplementary Note 12). The prespecified primary test, the fixed projection against matched random gene sets within disease in GSE162694 and GSE213621 with Holm correction over the two cohorts, missed: rho = 0.547 against a null 95th percentile of 0.528 (109 samples) and 0.465 against 0.457 (293 samples), Holm *p* = 0.082 in each. The cohort-PC1 score exceeded its null (*p* = 0.001), but the prespecification does not allow PC1 to replace the primary score. Matched random gene sets give a mean of 86 continuum-associated programs against 92 observed (*p* = 0.109). These checks were computed on an earlier 844-record version of the bulk data and were not rerun on the 774 units. Further screening of public data did not identify a new, eligible independent validation cohort with verified donor linkage.

Taken together, these analyses place cross-sectional stage-associated remodeling in donor-aware cell-type and continuous molecular context. They do not redefine the program registry, and they do not treat histologic groups or the continuum as states that change over time within a person. Colocalization gives no positive genetic call for IGFBP7 or BICC1, and the unresolved ambient-RNA sensitivity prevents assigning either program to hepatocytes from these data alone.
