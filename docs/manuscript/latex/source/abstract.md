# Summary

Human genetic studies and analyses of diseased tissue provide different views of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), but connecting these observations to experiments remains challenging. Here, we assemble a human MASLD resource that brings liver-trait genetics together with disease-associated gene expression and cell and tissue context. Multicohort liver transcriptomics identifies metabolic and multicellular remodeling associated with established disease and fibrosis stage. Lower expression of GNMT, MAT1A and CYP2C19 is accompanied by lower liver protein abundance, linking the tissue transcriptome to metabolic changes at the protein level. Genetic nominations arise predominantly from liver-enzyme traits and remain provisional where variant assignments are unresolved. Their overlap with disease-associated expression does not establish enrichment or depletion, underscoring the need to interpret each source of evidence on its own terms. We organize these observations in the MASLD Gene Catalog, which records the evidence available for each gene, identifies missing measurements and proposes experiments to resolve the remaining questions. This resource provides a framework for studying inherited susceptibility and established liver disease and for designing functional follow-up in relevant biological contexts.
