# Abstract — working Resource-paper draft

> Scope and numerical status: follow `docs/PAPER.md`, `docs/STATUS.md`, and
> `docs/RESULTS.md`. Exact genetics and genetics–expression overlap values remain
> withheld until the corrected COLOC release is audited and adopted.

Inherited genetic variation and the molecular state of established disease
capture different dimensions of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic
liver disease (MASLD), but they have not been connected systematically across
human cohorts, liver cell populations, and tissue contexts. We assembled a
multimodal human MASLD Resource spanning 1,257 quality-controlled bulk
RNA-sequencing samples from nine cohorts, including 844 samples from five
control-bearing cohorts in a pooled cohort-adjusted analysis, together with
phenotype- and ancestry-qualified GWAS, fine-mapping, and liver-eQTL
colocalization. The genetic and disease-state maps were complementary rather
than interchangeable. A worked GNMT example connects a provisional direct
disease shared-signal locus to a severity-linked GNMT–MAT1A–CYP2C19 state,
defined by coordinated decreases across disease-state RNA, fibrosis-stage
contrasts, and liver protein. This bridge does not imply that all three genes
share one inherited mechanism. An externally anchored molecular continuum
complemented recorded fibrosis stage, preserving the major metabolic,
inflammatory, ductular, and extracellular-matrix patterns while resolving
coordinated transcriptional variation among participants assigned the same
stage. Donor-aware single-cell analysis and a prespecified 117-program registry
resolved remodeling into multicellular programs while ambient-RNA sensitivity
limited lineage assignment. Protein,
chromatin-accessibility, and spatial assays
placed candidate genes and disease programs in assay-native molecular and
physical tissue contexts. Matched spatial nulls distinguished an ECM/IGFBP7
program with program-specific tissue organization from a
ductular-injury/BICC1 program whose spatial pattern did not exceed matched
background. The contrast separates disease association, tissue architecture,
and cellular origin rather than treating them as interchangeable evidence.
The MASLD Gene Catalog connects protein-coding genes, lncRNA
transcripts, regulatory DNA elements, and unresolved loci to their genetic,
disease-state, cell, and tissue contexts and to the next discriminating
experiment. Explicit observability, provenance, and evidence-state annotations
prevent missing or inapplicable measurements from being interpreted as
negative evidence. This Resource provides a reusable map from inherited
variation to multicellular MASLD biology and the experiments needed to resolve
mechanism.
